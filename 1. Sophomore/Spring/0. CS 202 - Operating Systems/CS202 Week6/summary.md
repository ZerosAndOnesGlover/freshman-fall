# CS 202 · Week 6 · Summary

**Topic —** What happens when memory runs out: swapping and major faults, page-replacement policies simulated and measured, working sets, thrashing, overcommit, and the OOM killer.
**Lectures —** L19 Swapping, and the Cost of a Major Fault; L20 Choosing a Victim: Page Replacement; L21 Working Sets, Thrashing, and the OOM Killer.
**Work —** PS 5 due Friday; PS 6 (100, released Wednesday, due Friday of Week 7); Quiz 6 (Monday, covers Week 5); Lab 5 (sat Tuesday of this week); Lab 6 (unmarked, sat Tuesday of Week 7).
**Takeaway —** xv6 answers exhaustion by refusing: `sbrk` returned −1 after 56,735 pages, `fork` returned −1, and the shell kept running. Linux instead reclaims — dropping clean file pages, writing dirty ones back, and swapping anonymous pages to a 4 GiB file on NVMe — and a page read back cost 90 µs, fifty minor faults or eleven thousand cache hits, with the stall showing up as 14–18% I/O pressure against 6% memory pressure. Which page to take back is the policy: on the textbook string FIFO took 15 faults, Clock 14, LRU 12 and OPT 9; FIFO and Clock both faulted more with four frames than three, while LRU and OPT cannot, being stack algorithms. On a generated string whose working set was 50 pages, the policies were indistinguishable below it and 68% apart above it; on the memory trace of a real `sort`, Clock came within 10% of LRU from 32 frames up, and 16 frames were enough to drop below one fault per hundred references. A working set is measurable from an ordinary account — a 257 MiB process showed 16 MiB — and thrashing is a cliff: 12% too little memory cost 18× throughput and 75% too little cost 1,470×, while the same shortage with a hot 32 MiB region cost only 11× that much. Overcommit refused a single 12 GiB mapping and accepted 8 TB in 8 GiB pieces; `RLIMIT_AS` is the limit an ordinary user can actually set. The OOM killer's score is linear in memory held and in `oom_score_adj` — which the session's own systemd floors at 100 — and a kill inside a 64 MiB scope arrived at 48 MiB as `SIGKILL`, recorded in `memory.events` and the journal.
