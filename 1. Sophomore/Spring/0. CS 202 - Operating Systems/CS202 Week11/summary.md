**Takeaway —** Three assumptions this course relied on fail at once across machines: nothing is shared, failures are partial and indistinguishable from slowness, and there is no common clock — measured here as a `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` eight hours ahead of `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` because of suspend. Distance is the dominant cost: a function call 1.8 ns, a system call 0.84 µs, a pipe round trip 8.5–10.5 µs, loopback UDP and TCP 19–22 µs, and a round trip to another machine 22.6–24.5 ms — thirteen million times the function call — so algorithms are judged in round trips. A successful `write()` means nothing about delivery: 2,643,968 bytes were accepted for a peer that had stopped reading, which is why unacknowledged operations must be idempotent and identifiable. Raft makes agreement from these materials by electing one leader per term: a follower that hears no heartbeat for a randomised 150–300 ms timeout stands for election, each node grants at most one vote per term, and a majority wins — so two leaders in one term is impossible by counting, which 200 simulated runs confirmed. Measured, a clean five-node cluster elected a leader at tick 170; a crashed leader cost 232 leaderless ticks of which 219 were merely waiting to notice; a partition produced two leaders in different terms for 756 ticks, with only the majority side able to commit, the older stepping down 47 ticks after the heal; and message loss degraded the cluster abruptly rather than gradually — invisible to 20%, thrashing at 40% with 19.8 elections, and effectively dead at 80% with 85.6 elections producing 0.4 leaders and 97.7% of time leaderless.
