# PROG 202 · Week 5 · Summary

**Topic —** Monads: the plumbing pattern named. `Maybe` and `Either` as monads, `do` as punctuation for `>>=`, `State` as a function rather than a container, and `IO` as `State RealWorld`.
**Lectures —** L11 Monads: The Pattern You Have Already Written Three Times; L12 The `State` Monad, `IO`, and the Leak That Has Two Names.
**Work —** PS 5 (100, fifteen parts, due Friday of Week 6), Quiz 5 (Tuesday, covers Week 4). **Lab 4 is sat this Wednesday**; Lab 5 is sat Wednesday of Week 6, **the day before the midterm**. **Required reading: Wadler, *Monads for Functional Programming* (1992) — §1–3 before Tuesday, §4–5 before Thursday, on the final.**
**Takeaway —** You had already written the plumbing three times: Project 1's `case … of Left e -> Left e` chains, Project 1's hand-rolled `filterM'`, and Lab 1's `traverse id`. `>>=` is that pattern named, and for `Maybe` and `Either e` its definition is two lines — **`Either e`, not `Either`, because a monad's parameter must take exactly one more argument, which is why the error type is fixed and `Right` means success as a matter of kinds rather than convention.** `do` desugars mechanically to `>>=`, `>>` and `let`, so **`<-` is the parameter of a lambda `>>=` is about to be given** — which is finally what Week 0 meant by "not assignment". `Functor` → `Applicative` → `Monad` are each superclasses, so `return` and `>>=` alone **do not compile** on GHC 9.4.7 (`No instance for (Applicative Box) arising from the superclasses of an instance declaration`) — the third and last of this course's three disagreements with *Real World Haskell*. Once named, the plumbing is library code: **`filterM` is their `filterM'` and `sequence` is Lab 1's `traverse id`** — substituting it leaves the output byte-identical, checked. And `sequence [[1,2],[3,4]]` is **`[[1,3],[1,4],[2,3],[2,4]]`**, the Cartesian product, which is Week 9's execution model seen early. **`State s a` is `s -> (a, s)`** — a function, not a container — and `>>=` threads the state, so **`State` is a fold with the accumulator hidden**. Lab 5's finding is that writing the threading by hand and passing `t1` where `t2` was meant **is caught by nothing**, because every state has the same type: the monad's value is that there is no `t2` to confuse with `t1`. `IO` is `State RealWorld` **with `runState` missing**, and that missing function is the whole of its privilege; `unsafePerformIO` is it, provided anyway. Then Week 3's question for the third time, counting to three million at `-O0`: `Lazy`+`modify` **308 MB**, `Lazy`+`modify'` **398 MB — worse by being more strict**, `Strict`+`modify` **167 MB**, `Strict`+`modify'` **44 KB**; all four are 44 KB at `-O2`. **`State.Strict` forces the `(a, s)` pair and `modify'` forces the state value — orthogonal, and you need both.** In the students' own interpreter the same change is a more realistic 2× (9.78 MB against 17.5 MB), because the expression tree puts a floor under it.
**New syntax —** `>>=` `>>` `=<<` · `<-` re-explained · `pure`/`return` · `{-# LANGUAGE LambdaCase #-}`; and `mapM` `mapM_` `filterM` `foldM` `sequence` `when` `unless` `replicateM` `void` `State` `get` `put` `modify` **`modify'`** `gets` `evalState` `execState` `runState` `Control.Monad.State.Strict`.
**Next —** Week 6 — applicative functors, monad transformers, and functional error handling: how to have `State` *and* `Either` at once, and what `traverse` has been all along. **The midterm is that Thursday, covering Weeks 0–5.**
