**Takeaway —** `>>=` is too strong for independent work: three independent checks over `Either` report **one error**, because `m >>= f` hands `f` the *result* of `m` and a `Left` has none — short-circuiting is the definition, not a defect. **`<*>`'s second argument is a value, not a function**, so its two arguments cannot depend on each other, and *because* they cannot both can run: the same three checks over a hand-written `Validation` applicative report **all three** (`1 error(s)` against `3 error(s)`, measured), and the instance needs `Semigroup e` because the both-failed case combines errors with `<>` — Week 4's class doing the work. **And `Validation` must not be a `Monad`:** the instance compiles `-Wall` silent, and breaks the law that every monad's `<*>` agrees with its `ap` — `Left ["e1","e2"]` against `Left ["e1"]`, checked. So a type can accumulate or be a monad, not both, which is why `Applicative` is a separate class rather than a step towards `Monad`, and why `traverse`'s constraint is `Applicative`: the same traversal gives first-failure over `Either` and every failure over `Validation`, and an applicative traversal is not obliged to be sequential (Week 7). `traverse id` is `sequenceA`, which is what Lab 1 was doing all along. Monads do not compose, so a **transformer** is a monad parameterised by another: `StateT s m a = s -> m (a, s)`, and `State s = StateT s Identity` (checked). A state you never modify is a **`Reader`** — Project 1's `Env`. The `mtl` classes `MonadState`/`MonadError`/`MonadReader` mean you write no `lift`, **and that is exactly why the week's trap is silent**: measured, `StateT Int (Either String)` gives `Left "boom at 3"` and **loses the count**, while `ExceptT String (State Int)` gives **`(Left "boom at 3", 3)`** — the error *and* how far it got — from a byte-identical program. **The rule: what must survive a failure goes outside `ExceptT`**, and it falls straight out of `runStateT :: … -> m (a, s)` against `runExceptT :: … -> m (Either e a)`. Lab 6 ships its stack in the wrong order so that the students diagnose it from seven identically-shaped failures (right error, `Trace 0 0`) — 6 of 13 passing until one line changes. **This is the third silent type-level choice of Weeks 3–6 and the worst:** `Data.Map.Lazy` (107 MB) and `State.Lazy` (398 MB) cost *performance* and compute the same answer; this one costs *semantics*.
