# CS 212 · Week 4 · Summary

**Topic —** Design patterns: where they came from, what a pattern is, which of the twenty-three survive in Python, and refactoring toward one rather than starting from one.
**Lectures —** L13 Patterns as a Vocabulary; L14 Creational and Structural Patterns, in Production Code; L15 Behavioural Patterns, and the Ones That Became Language Features.
**Work —** A 3 due Friday 17:00; A 4 released Wednesday (100, a pull request plus a paper, due Friday of Week 5); **Quiz 4 Tuesday** (unmarked, covers Week 3).
**Takeaway —** Alexander's definition has three load-bearing clauses — a recurring **problem**, the *core* of a solution, never the same twice — and the first is why **a pattern is named for its problem**, which is how Decorator and Proxy can share a structure and be two patterns. The Gang of Four's durable contribution is a vocabulary mined from real systems, and its own warning that patterns add indirection is in §1.8 and is never quoted. **Norvig, 1996: 16 of the 23 are invisible or simpler in a dynamic language** — Strategy is a function argument, Command a closure, Iterator `yield`, Singleton a module — **so learn patterns as problems, because the problems survive and the structures do not.** Pattern-oriented programming is the failure mode: six correct classes to construct three fields, and the three tests against it are *name the problem, name the second case, count the files a reader must open*. Creational patterns exist because construction is a decision that changes for different reasons than use, and `roomsvc` builds an `SMTPMailer` inside `confirm_booking` in eleven places; **the test data builder is the week's highest-value pattern**, because a test stating six facts to assert one is a test you will delete. Singleton is a global variable with a nicer name — untestable, a hidden dependency, the wrong lifetime — and the alternative costs three characters at the call site. **Adapter is irreducible engineering**, because the work is the translation: `roomsvc` has none, and `employeeType == 'E'` in four files locked research staff out for five months. Observer's content is the many-to-one registration, and four things bite — a raising handler kills the rest, order gets relied on, you lose the ability to read the control flow, and **publishing inside a transaction re-creates the original bug with the arrow reversed.** For three states and four transitions a transition table beats both the `if` tree and the State pattern, visible in four lines and exhaustively testable in nine cases. **Inheritance is very strong coupling — connascence of execution order across a class boundary, invisible in any signature** — and `roomsvc`'s `BaseReport` has two subclasses that override the template itself. **The week's portable sentence: a pattern is a workaround for something the language cannot yet say, and languages learn** — eight of the twenty-three are now syntax, so the patterns still worth the name are the ones that have not dissolved.
