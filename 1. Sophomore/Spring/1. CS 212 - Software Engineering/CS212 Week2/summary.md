# CS 212 · Week 2 · Summary

**Topic —** Design principles: cohesion and coupling; SOLID rated letter by letter against code that exists; DRY, YAGNI and separation of concerns, with the cases where each is wrong.
**Lectures —** L07 Coupling and Cohesion — the Two Ideas the Rest Are About; L08 SOLID, One Letter at a Time, in Code That Exists; L09 DRY, YAGNI, Separation of Concerns — and When Each Is Wrong.
**Work —** A 1 due Friday 17:00; A 2 released Wednesday (100, a pull request plus an argument, due Friday of Week 3); **Quiz 2 Tuesday** (unmarked, covers Week 1).
**Takeaway —** `confirm_booking` is not bad because it is 487 lines: split it into nine four-line functions and every real problem survives, because the disease is low cohesion and high coupling rather than length. Cohesion is measurable without judgement — `bookings.py` takes **891 of 2,173 file-touches in two years, 41%**, because eight unrelated concerns live in it, and a cohesive module is touched when its one concern changes. Stamp coupling makes a function untestable, unscriptable and unreusable; content coupling — `admin.py:271` setting `state` directly — dissolves another module's invariants. Parnas (1972) got the decomposition criterion right in four pages: split by what is likely to change, hide each likely change in one module, **and know which decisions cannot be hidden**, because those are the ones you must get right. Connascence makes coupling actionable: nine ordered forms, each with a standard weakening, and the rule that stronger connascence may span shorter distances. SOLID is a 2004 mnemonic of unequal parts — **S** is useful in the *one actor* form (`bookings.py` serves five, which is how a VAT change shipped a permission regression), **O** only against a demonstrated axis of change (the pricing tree is switched on in four places and commit `7b1e4f2` missed one for eight months — but the same abstraction in 2020 would have been speculative generality), **L** is the only letter with formal content and `Equipment.book` violates it in production, **I** is weak in Python as stated and better restated as *the smallest interface you can test against*, and **D** is the most valuable for a reason that is not the one usually given: it buys fast tests and a readable domain, not portability. Four of the five reduce to coupling and cohesion. DRY is about **knowledge, not code** — `range(8, 20)` in four places is real duplication with three of four updated in 2023, while two lookalike validators merged in 2022 caused an incident in 2025 — and the test is whether one change to the world would require both to change, always, in the same way. YAGNI has four costs, of which *blocking* is the worst, and a plugin system with 380 lines, 31 commits and zero plugins is the evidence; **it does not apply to the security model, migrations, the audit trail, observability or legal deadlines**, because those cannot be retrofitted proportionally. **The week is one idea with four names — what changes together? — and the answer is in the `git log`, not in your intuition.**
