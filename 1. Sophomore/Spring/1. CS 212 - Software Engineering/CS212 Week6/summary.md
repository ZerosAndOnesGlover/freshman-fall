**Takeaway —** A suite with 100% line and branch coverage and zero assertions is four lines to construct and stays green under every mutation, because **coverage measures execution, not checking**. Enabling branch coverage costs one configuration line and takes `roomsvc` from 61.0% to **48.3%**, with the whole gap in error paths — `bookings.py` drops from 47.1% to 31.8% and has **198 partially-covered branches**. Coverage answers one question well — *which code has no test at all* — and its legitimate uses are finding untested regions, gating the **diff**, and acting as a **ratchet**; a **target** is Goodhart's law in textbook form, and every way of hitting it is rational and useless. **Inozemtseva & Holmes (ICSE 2014), 31,000 suites: coverage correlates with effectiveness only through suite size, and the correlation largely vanishes when size is controlled.** Read a report bottom-first — high coverage on a logic-free models file means nothing — then top-first against change frequency, then `BrPart`, and never the total. Even at 100%, coverage cannot see missing code, wrong values, **concurrency** (the VNC 101 race was in covered code), missing assertions, a wrong specification, integration, or data-dependent paths. Property-based testing inverts example selection, because **examples come from the same mental model that produced the code**; the five patterns are invariants, **round-trip** (most bugs per line), an **oracle** (how you optimise safely), the inverse, and **idempotence**; and **shrinking is the feature** — Hypothesis reduces a failure to a two-occurrence rule starting 01:30 on 29 March 2026, the hour the clocks go forward, which is issue #31, open since 2020. A `RuleBasedStateMachine` generates operation sequences and checks the invariant after each, **and cannot find the race**, because it runs against the fake in one process. **Mutation testing reconciles 61% and 31%** with three survivor types — no assertion, asserting the route not the result, and a branch covered one way — **and the third survivor is the VNC 101 bug, generated as a mutant, against which all 212 tests passed.** Equivalent mutants are undecidable to detect and are 5–20% of survivors, so 100% is not a target: **read individual survivors, do not optimise the aggregate.** **Just et al. (FSE 2014), 357 real faults: mutant kill rate predicts real-fault detection after controlling for coverage** — which, with the first paper, is why **95% coverage with a 30% mutation score scores below 70% with 75%**. Make it tractable by mutating the diff, running only covering tests, and keeping the suite fast, since mutation time ≈ mutants × suite time.
