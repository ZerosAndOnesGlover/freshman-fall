## For After 1 May — the three books

**In the order they will be useful, not the order they were written.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. Feathers, *Working Effectively with Legacy Code*** | **Read it in the first fortnight of your first job**, while staring at something you cannot change. It will be the most useful book on your shelf for two years |
| **2. Ousterhout, *A Philosophy of Software Design*** | ~190 pages, opinionated, and **the best available counter-argument to *Clean Code*** — which this course disagreed with in five places and which your colleagues will have read as scripture |
| **3. Winters, Manshreck & Wright, *Software Engineering at Google*** | The best writing on engineering **over time, at scale**. Free online. **Read it once you have been somewhere long enough to be irritated by something** |

**And one more, if you found Week 11 the most interesting week:** **Tornhill, *Software Design X-Rays*** —
hotspots, change coupling and knowledge distribution at organisational scale.

## What Not to Read

**Any book with "clean", "agile" or "craftsmanship" in the title, for at least a year.** Not because they
are worthless — several are on this course's own list — but because **you have just spent thirteen weeks
learning to ask for the cost, the context and the evidence, and that genre is written in a register that
discourages all three.** Come back to them when the habit is solid enough to survive them.

**And nothing at all in the week before the final.** §1 of the revision guide is the plan; adding
material is not revision.

## The Habit, Rather Than the Reading

**L39 §3's recommendation, restated because it is the one thing on this page that compounds:**

> **Keep a decision log of your own.** Not for an employer — for you. What you decided, what you
> rejected, what you expected to happen. Then read it in a year.

**It is an ADR pointed at yourself**, and it is the only instrument you have for finding out whether
your engineering judgement is actually improving. **Thirteen weeks of this course argued that a decision
without its rejected alternative is unrecoverable, and that applies to your own.**
