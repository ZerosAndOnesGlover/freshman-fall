### The one rule

**No team may consist entirely of people who have worked together before.** At least two members of every team must be new to each other.

This is not team-building for its own sake. **The thing this course is about — that code must be readable by someone who did not write it and was not in the conversation — does not happen in a group of four friends who already share every assumption.** Half the value of the project is discovering which of your conventions are conventions rather than facts.

## Forming Well

**Balance the hours column, not the skills column.** Teams fail over availability far more often than over ability. A team of four with fifteen hours each beats a team with one excellent member who has three.

**Ask directly, in the room:**

- *"What else are you taking?"* — CS 202's Project 1 lands in Week 7 and is due Week 11. ECE 211 and MATH 251 both hold midterms in the same three days as CS 212's. Two people with identical timetables have identical crunch weeks.
- *"Have you used git in a team before?"* — not a filter. A team where everyone answers no should know that, and should spend an hour in Week 1 on it.
- *"What do you want out of this?"* — a team where two people want to learn and two want a good mark without much work is a team that will discover this in April.

**Team size: 4 or 5.** Five is not better. Five has ten communication pairs against six for four (L01 §2), and five people cannot all be doing something useful on a walking skeleton.

## Week 1, For the Avoidance of Doubt

By **Friday of Week 1** your team owes, in the repository:

- `docs/charter.md`, filled in
- **A walking skeleton**: one booking created and read back through every layer
- A CI workflow that runs the tests on push — even if there is one test and it asserts `True`

**None of this is marked in Week 1.** All of it is 70% of Phase 1 in Week 6, and the skeleton in particular is the thing every previous cohort wished they had done now.
