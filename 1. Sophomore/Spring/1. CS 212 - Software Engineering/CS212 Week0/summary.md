# CS 212 · Week 0 · Summary

**Topic —** Software engineering as a discipline: the software crisis and what named it; process models as bets on feedback latency; the Agile Manifesto read as four trade-offs rather than four commandments.
**Lectures —** L01 Why Software Engineering Was Invented; L02 Process Models, and What They Were Reacting To; L03 The Agile Manifesto, Read Critically.
**Work —** A 0 (100, 1,200–1,800 words, due Friday of Week 1), Team Formation Workshop (unmarked, Thursday of Week 0). **No quiz** — Quiz 1 in Week 1 covers this week.
**Takeaway —** The phrase "software engineering" was coined at NATO Garmisch in October 1968 as a deliberate provocation, naming a discipline that did not exist; the crisis it named is an economics problem, since cost grows faster than linearly in size and Brooks's $n(n-1)/2$ communication paths are one reason. Four of the six canonical disasters — Therac-25, London Ambulance, FBI VCF, Knight Capital — failed on process rather than code, and Ariane 5 failed on correct code whose assumptions had silently moved. About 60% of lifetime cost falls after first release and only ~21% of that is bug-fixing, so the thing being optimised is changeability, not correctness. Every process model since 1968 is a bet on how long you go before finding out you were wrong, and the sequence project → release → loop → sprint → item → minutes is the whole history; Royce's 1970 paper rejects its own Figure 2 on page 329 and recommends building it twice. Boehm's ~100× cost curve is real but partly a fact about 1981's feedback loops, and the better argument for short cycles is that ~2/3 of delivered features are rarely or never used and no analysis says which third. The Manifesto's four lines are trade-offs whose closing sentence — *"there is value in the items on the right"* — is the one people delete; "individuals over processes" is the wrong instruction for deployment, and Knight Capital's missed eighth server is why. The reference codebase `roomsvc` is 11,438 lines, 2,847 commits, one surviving author of five, a 487-line function with cyclomatic complexity 94, **61% line coverage and a 31% mutation score** — and its 14 Oct 2024 double-booking of VNC 101 took 23 days to diagnose and four months to ship a fourteen-line fix, because nobody could convince themselves that touching that function was safe.
