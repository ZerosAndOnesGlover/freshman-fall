# CS 211 · Week 8 · Summary

**Topic —** Type systems: Hindley-Milner over real terms, the occurs check, inferred against declared, three polymorphisms, let-polymorphism and rank, System F and strong normalisation, parametricity, type classes as dictionary passing, and the Curry-Howard correspondence.
**Lectures —** L17 What Types Fix, and What They Do Not; L18 System F, Dictionaries, and Proofs as Programs.
**Work —** **MIDTERM 2** (Tuesday 20:00–21:15, 75 marks, 12.5%, covers Weeks 4–7), PS 8 (100, due Week 9 Friday), Lab 8 (unmarked, Friday), Quiz 8 (unmarked, Tuesday morning, covers Week 7).
**Takeaway —** Week 3's inference engine, run unchanged on Week 7's prelude, rejects 16 of 55 definitions and **every rejection is the occurs check** — including `and` and `or`, because Church conjunction is self-application in disguise and computes conjunction correctly on all four inputs anyway. The headline is a correction: **types do not fix Week 7's collision.** `zero`, `false` and `nil` all infer to `∀a b. a → b → b`, and the three "theorems" share their types too; a principal type is a function of the term, so inference adds nothing. What separates them is a *declaration* — the useful axis is inferred against declared, not typed against untyped, and Week 7's files were corrected to say so. Measured alongside: `let` is sugar for evaluation and not for typing, so `(λi. i i) (λx. x)` reduces to the identity and is rejected — sound and incomplete, the same shape as Week 5's may/must and Week 6's reference counting; deleting `generalise`'s environment guard leaves the program typeable with a *wrong* type that lets one value be used at two types, which is the opposite failure; and `curry.py` proves 12 intuitionistic tautologies with `id`, `const` and the S combinator as the first three proofs, decides five classical ones unprovable, and type-checks its own proof terms — a guard added after a genuine arity bug survived every provability test.
