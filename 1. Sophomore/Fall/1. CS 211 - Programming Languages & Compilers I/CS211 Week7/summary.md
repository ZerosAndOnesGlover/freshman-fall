# CS 211 · Week 7 · Summary

**Topic —** The untyped lambda calculus: three constructs, capture-avoiding substitution, alpha/beta/eta, normal forms and undecidability, four reduction strategies, Church encodings, fixed-point combinators, de Bruijn indices, Turing equivalence.
**Lectures —** L15 Three Constructs, and the One That Is Hard; L16 Encodings, Recursion, and Why Y Hangs in Python.
**Work —** PS 7 (100, due Week 8 Friday), Lab 7 (unmarked, Friday), Quiz 7 (unmarked, Tuesday, covers Week 6). **Midterm 2 next week, Weeks 4–7.**
**Takeaway —** `zero`, `false` and `nil` are literally the same term, and brute force over the prelude finds seven such pairs — three of them theorems any encoding would reproduce (`compose == mult`, `const == true`, `apply == one`) and four of them accidents; telling those apart turns out to need a *declaration* rather than an inference, which is Week 8's measurement. Measured alongside: naive substitution turns a constant function into the identity in one beta-step with no error, yet capture avoidance fires 0 times on most terms, so de Bruijn's real case is correctness and not speed (identical beta counts, 5–74× the per-step cost); normal order is complete and unusable — 10,384 reductions for `fact 4` against call-by-value's 552 and call-by-need's 384 — and only sharing reconciles the theorem with the measurement; `Y` diverges under call-by-value in 44 steps, `Z` survives 3911 and still fails because `if` is a function that evaluates both branches, and only thunking works; `eta(Z)` really is `Y`, verified, so two terms that are the same function differ in whether they terminate. All of it reproduced in Python, importing nothing from the interpreter.
