# CS 211 · Week 6 · Summary

**Topic —** Runtime systems: stack vs heap, manual `free` and its two failure modes, reference counting, tri-colour mark-and-sweep, root sets from liveness, precise vs conservative, generations, write barriers, and reading a real GC log.
**Lectures —** L13 The Instruction Nothing Frees; L14 Roots, Generations, and the Cost of a Write Barrier.
**Work —** PS 6 (100, due Week 7 Friday), Lab 6 (unmarked, Friday), Quiz 6 (unmarked, Tuesday, covers Week 5). **Project 1 assigned, due Week 11.**
**Takeaway —** The collector's root set *is* Week 5's liveness, and nothing checks it: the same def/use bug that deleted an instruction in Week 5 now frees `#2` and writes through it, while rooting everything in scope instead costs 13.5× residency and 7.8× marking on the same program. Measured alongside: Cyan could not express a heap cycle at all — three unrelated type rules, none designed for it — until eleven lines of bidirectional checking leaked 80% of the heap; reference counting has the *smallest* footprint here (8 objects peak against mark-sweep's 16) and is the only collector that can measure a lifetime, since the tracer's median age of 33 is just half its own threshold of 64; the promotion hole no write barrier can cover freed 97 reachable objects while printing the right answer, then crashed once a reader walked the list; 538 collections reclaimed zero objects until a growth policy cut it to 4; and `-Xlog:gc` reported ZGC's worst pause as 8 ms when it is 115 µs — 70× wrong, in the direction that makes G1 look 14× better than it is.
