# CS 211 · Week 10 · Summary

**Topic —** Metaprogramming and domain-specific languages: macros against functions, homoiconicity, quasiquote, variable capture and hygiene, internal against external DSLs, parser combinators, macro systems across languages, and code generation.
**Lectures —** L21 Code as Data, and the Macro That Is `if`; L22 Little Languages, and What They Cost.
**Work —** PS 10 (100, due Week 11 Friday), Lab 10 (unmarked, Friday), Quiz 10 (unmarked, Tuesday, covers Week 9). **Project 1 due Week 11 Friday — the same day as PS 10.**
**Takeaway —** Week 7 proved `if` cannot be a function under call-by-value and fixed it with thunks at the caller; a macro fixes it at the definition, because it runs at expansion time on unevaluated syntax — `(unless-fn #t (boom))` raises and `(unless-mac #t (boom))` returns `nil`, same idea, two mechanisms. `while` then becomes six lines of library code with no change to the evaluator, and no macro survives expansion, exactly like Week 8's dictionaries. Week 7's capture bug arrives shipped: `(swap-bad tmp z)` gives `(7 7)` instead of `(7 100)` and expands to `(define tmp tmp)`, and `gensym` — which is Week 7's `fresh` renamed — fixes it. The week's honest result is a cost rather than a win: parser combinators shrink the arithmetic grammar from 357 lines to **4** and make it a value whose associativity lives in a fold, and they inherit Week 2's left recursion unchanged *and* degrade the diagnostics from `line 1 col 30: expected ')', found ';'` to `expected number or ')' at '(1 + 2'` — which is why clang, rustc and GHC all hand-write their parsers. Also measured: the internal and external circuit DSLs produce identical truth tables, and a full adder is 5 gates, not 6, once the counter treats a shared subexpression as one wire.
