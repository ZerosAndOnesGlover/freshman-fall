**Takeaway —** A linear system has one of three outcomes and never a fourth, because two distinct solutions generate a whole line of them. Elimination finds out which, without being told in advance: count the pivots for the matrix, read the last rows for whether this $b$ is reachable. It costs $\approx n^3/3$ against back substitution's $n^2/2$ — so reaching triangular form is the expensive part, which is why Week 1 keeps the factorisation, and why PageRank at $n = 10^9$ would need $3\times10^{26}$ operations and is not solved this way. The exactness is arithmetic, not algorithmic: exact elimination on a random $100\times100$ matrix of single digits produced a pivot needing **299 decimal digits**, and in floating point the same algorithm returns $x_1 = 0.0$ where the answer is $1$ — traced to the step where $1 - 10^{17}$ and $2 - 10^{17}$ round to the same `double`. Partial pivoting fixes it by bounding every multiplier at $1$, for $O(n^2)$ comparisons, and every production solver does it unconditionally. But a matrix can be invertible and still hopeless: $\operatorname{cond}_\infty(H_{12}) = 4.1\times10^{16}$ yields **two correct digits out of sixteen** from a perfect algorithm, and $\det H_{10} = 2.2\times10^{-53}$ diagnoses nothing, because the determinant changes with your choice of units and the condition number does not.
