# MATH 241 · Week 8 · Summary

**Topic —** Orthogonality, projections, orthonormal bases and Gram–Schmidt.
**Lectures —** L24 Orthogonality; L25 Projections; L26 Orthonormal Bases and Gram–Schmidt.
**Work —** PS 8 (100, released Wednesday, due Friday of Week 9), Quiz 8 (Monday, covers Week 7, unmarked). **PS 7 due Friday.** Recitation 7 is sat Thursday and covers Week 7; Recitation 8 covers this week and is sat Thursday of Week 9. **PROG 201's and CS 211's Midterm 2 both fall this week**; MATH 241's is Week 10, covering Weeks 6–9.
**Takeaway —** Week 2 built vector spaces with no length and no angle and named this week as the one that would supply them; the intervening six weeks — bases, dimension, rank, the four subspaces, determinants, eigenvalues, diagonalisation — turned out to need neither. Adding the dot product settles four outstanding debts at once. **The four subspaces are orthogonal in pairs**, proved in one line: $Ax = 0$ says every entry of $Ax$ vanishes, and entry $i$ is (row $i$)$\cdot x$, so $x \perp$ every row — which Week 3 could only verify on one matrix. They are moreover orthogonal **complements**, and that stronger word is what Week 3's REC 3 §2(d) showed dimension arithmetic alone cannot give; it also supplies **the converse Week 3's L12 §5 owed**, so the solvability conditions on $b$ are sufficient as well as necessary. **The nearest point of a subspace to $b$ is $p$, where $b = p + e$ with $e \perp W$** — proved by Pythagoras — and requiring the error to be orthogonal to every column gives the **normal equations** $A^\mathsf{T}A\hat x = A^\mathsf{T}b$, invertible by **PS 2 Q5(c)**, used here for the first time since Week 2. The resulting $P = A(A^\mathsf{T}A)^{-1}A^\mathsf{T}$ satisfies $P^2 = P$ and $P^\mathsf{T} = P$, closing loops opened in Weeks 1, 4 and 7, and **a projection's eigenvalues are $1$ on $W$ and $0$ on $W^\perp$**, so its trace counts dimensions. Gram–Schmidt makes any basis orthonormal by subtracting projections; on $1, x, x^2$ sampled it produces the **discrete Legendre vectors** $(1,1,1)$, $(-1,0,1)$, $(1,-2,1)$, and Week 12's Fourier construction is the same procedure on functions. Recording the coefficients gives **$A = QR$, the fourth factorisation of the course and the first with no hypothesis beyond independent columns.** The point of all of it is **conditioning, not elegance**: Week 7 found $A = S\Lambda S^{-1}$ can exist and be worthless, with $\operatorname{cond}(S) = 1.3\times10^8$ for eigenvectors $(1,0)$ and $(1,10^{-8})$, while **$\operatorname{cond}(Q) = 1$ exactly** — multiplying by an orthonormal matrix cannot amplify a rounding error. That is why Weeks 9, 10 and 11 all insist on orthonormal bases, and why classical Gram–Schmidt is the right thing to understand and the wrong thing to run.
