**Takeaway —** $A = S\Lambda S^{-1}$ with the eigenvectors in the columns of $S$, proved in one line from $AS = S\Lambda$ read column by column — **it is Week 4's change of basis with $M = S$, and nothing else.** The hypothesis is the entire content: $S$ must be invertible, so the eigenvectors must be independent, so there must be $n$ of them, which is exactly what Week 6's defective matrices lack. What it buys is powers — $A^k = S\Lambda^kS^{-1}$ where $\Lambda^k$ is $n$ scalar powers, **a cost that does not grow with $k$**, against $O(\log k)$ matrix products for repeated squaring; and the same move gives $e^{At}$, which is how linear differential equations are solved. When it fails, **geometric $<$ algebraic**, and the proof that this is the only way to fail needs Week 3's basis extension, Week 4's change of basis and Week 5's block determinants — Week 7 introduces almost nothing and assembles everything. What sits in place of a missing eigenvector is *coupling*, $Se_2 = e_1 + 3e_2$, minimally expressed by a Jordan block; the Jordan form always exists and **nobody computes it**, because **defectiveness is not a matter of degree** — defective at $\varepsilon = 10^{-300}$ and diagonalisable at exactly $0$, with no continuous transition, so no algorithm can test for it and Weeks 10 and 11 avoid the question instead. Complex eigenvalues are normal rather than pathological: they arrive in conjugate pairs for a real matrix, a real $2\times2$ with eigenvalues $re^{\pm i\theta}$ is a rotation by $\theta$ scaled by $r$ with **$r = \sqrt{\det}$ and $\cos\theta = \operatorname{trace}/(2\sqrt{\det})$ readable without any eigenvector**, and $\lvert\lambda\rvert$ decides the fate of every iteration — the spectral radius is what "will this converge" means. Finally the applications, which are the same computation three times: **Binet's formula is $S\Lambda^kS^{-1}$ read off**, and the golden ratio is in the Fibonacci sequence because it is an eigenvalue; a Markov matrix always has $\lambda = 1$ with the steady state as its eigenvector; and **the mixing rate is $\lvert\lambda_2\rvert$** — which is why **PageRank is about fifty sparse matrix–vector products** and not the $3\times10^{26}$-operation elimination Week 0 ruled out.
