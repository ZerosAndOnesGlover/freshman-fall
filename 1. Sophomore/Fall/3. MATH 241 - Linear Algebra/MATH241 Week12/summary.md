**Takeaway —** Week 0's syllabus said the four applications are the same theorem, and the week tests the claim rather than repeating it. **Regression** is Week 9's projection, and its warning becomes a measurement: on 20 training points from $\sin 2.5t$ plus noise, training error falls at every degree while test error falls to $0.18$ — near the noise floor — and then rises to $16{,}412{,}119$ at degree 19; **the Legendre basis improves the conditioning a hundredfold and changes the test error not at all**, because a basis cannot change which subspace you chose — numerics and overfitting are different problems. **PCA** is the SVD of centred data: 300 points near a plane give 98.5% of the variance in two directions, recover the plane's normal to within $1.09°$, and lose exactly $\sigma_3^2$; **the regression plane through the same points is $5.09°$ away**, each winning the distance it minimises; and PCA through the covariance matrix is Week 9's normal-equations mistake again, **returning a negative variance** where the SVD resolves $10^{-18}$. **PageRank** is the $\lambda = 1$ eigenvector of a Markov matrix, repaired for dangling pages and damped to $G = 0.85P + 0.15/n$; on six pages it is exact in `Fraction`, page 0 ranks second on a single link because that link comes from the top page, and power iteration's error tracks $\lvert\lambda_2\rvert^k$ with $\lvert\lambda_2\rvert = 0.601$ (a complex pair, so the error spirals) against a measured $0.5875$ per step. **Without damping** a two-page cycle oscillates forever ($\lambda = -1$) and a split web has no unique ranking (a repeated $\lambda = 1$); damping makes $G$ positive, **Perron–Frobenius** gives a unique positive answer, and the bound $\lvert\lambda_2\rvert \le \alpha$ is attained exactly when the web splits; at $n = 10^9$ fifty sparse products are $6.7\times10^{14}$ times cheaper than elimination. **Fourier** is Week 8's orthonormal expansion in Week 2's space of functions: sines and cosines are orthogonal (to $6.8\times10^{-15}$ when sampled), coefficients are projections, and partial sums of a square wave have squared error falling like $1/K$ while **the peak converges to $1.178979744$ — an $18\%$ overshoot that never shrinks**, because projection controls squared error and a narrowing spike has vanishing area. **The DCT** is an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^8$ in which a smooth signal loses $2.5\%$ from three coefficients against $71\%$ from three samples — which is JPEG. **The verdict:** regression, PCA and Fourier are literally one theorem, orthogonal projection; **PageRank is not** — non-symmetric, complex eigenvalues, no inner product, well-posed only because positivity was engineered in. **What all four share is Week 4's program: find the basis in which the problem is simple — the same question, answered by the same theorem three times.** The course ends where its two habits began: every theorem an algorithm, and seven methods correct but not usable, from Cramer's rule to rank as a pivot count.
