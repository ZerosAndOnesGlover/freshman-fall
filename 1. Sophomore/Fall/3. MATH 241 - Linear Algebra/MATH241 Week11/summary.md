**Takeaway —** Every earlier factorisation bought its conclusion with a hypothesis — enough eigenvectors, independent columns, symmetry — and this one keeps every matrix and pays instead by using **a different orthonormal basis at each end**: $A = U\Sigma V^\mathsf{T}$ with $Av_k = \sigma_ku_k$, which Week 4's L14 §1 permitted and nothing until now needed. **The construction is Week 10 applied once**: $A^\mathsf{T}A$ is symmetric positive semidefinite for every $A$, so the spectral theorem gives $V$ and $\sigma_k^2$, and $u_k = Av_k/\sigma_k$ inherits orthonormality in one line; $AA^\mathsf{T}$ has the same nonzero eigenvalues and $U$ as its eigenvectors. Three matrices built backwards from $(3,4,5)$ and $(1,2,2)$ vectors have rational SVDs, checked **exactly in `Fraction`** — with the honest note that almost every matrix has irrational singular values. **Geometrically it is rotation, stretch, rotation**: the unit circle goes to an ellipse, and walking $360{,}000$ points round it measures $\max\lVert Ax\rVert = 20 = \sigma_1$, so $\lVert A\rVert_2 = \sigma_1$ — not the largest entry, not the largest eigenvalue, and **singular values are not eigenvalues** ($20, 5$ against $18.63, 5.37$). **$A = \sum\sigma_ku_kv_k^\mathsf{T}$** is Week 1's outer-product reading. **The SVD hands all four subspaces orthonormal bases at once**, and **row rank $=$ column rank is finally explained**: each nonzero $\sigma_k$ pairs a row-space direction with a column-space direction through $Av_k = \sigma_ku_k$ and $A^\mathsf{T}u_k = \sigma_kv_k$. **$\operatorname{cond}_2 = \sigma_1/\sigma_n$**, and $A^\mathsf{T}A = V(\Sigma^\mathsf{T}\Sigma)V^\mathsf{T}$ proves Week 9's squaring in one line — on Läuchli's matrix $\sigma_\min(A) = 10^{-8}$ is computed correctly and $\sigma_\min(A^\mathsf{T}A)$ comes out as exactly $0$. **The pseudoinverse $A^+ = V\Sigma^+U^\mathsf{T}$** satisfies the four Penrose conditions exactly, $AA^+$ and $A^+A$ are Week 8's projections, and **$x^+ = A^+b$ is the shortest least-squares solution** when the normal equations are singular — two predictors that always move together get credit split rather than invented. **Rank is a gap, not a count**: a rank-2 matrix plus $10^{-10}$ noise has six nonzero pivots and a singular-value gap of $8\times10^{10}$. **Eckart–Young** makes stopping the sum early optimal — $\lVert A - A_k\rVert_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$, measured to every digit, and $2000$ random competitors never beat it; a $24\times32$ picture has rank $7$, of which the bar and post cost $2$ and the ring costs $5$. **Total least squares** fits perpendicularly via the smallest singular vector of centred data, landing at slope $1.877$ between the two ordinary fits' $1.723$ and $1.925$ — the first appearance of PCA. The SVD survives everything Week 9's methods failed on and costs $42.9\times$ elimination as run, and it completes the course's spine: **five factorisations, and down the table the hypotheses fall away as the outer factors become orthogonal**.
