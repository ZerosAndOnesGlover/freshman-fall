# MATH 241 · Week 4 · Summary

**Topic —** Linear transformations, their matrices, and change of basis.
**Lectures —** L13 What a Linear Transformation Is; L14 The Matrix of a Transformation; L15 Change of Basis, and the Search for a Good One.
**Work —** PS 4 (100, released Wednesday, due Friday of Week 5), Quiz 4 (Monday, covers Week 3, unmarked). **PS 3 due Friday.** Recitation 3 is sat Thursday and covers Week 3; Recitation 4 covers this week and is sat Thursday of Week 5. **PROG 201's and CS 211's Midterm 1 both fall this week**; MATH 241's is Week 6.
**Takeaway —** A linear transformation is a function respecting addition and scaling — Week 0's two properties with vectors in place of numbers — and $T(0) = 0$ is the one-substitution test that catches most impostors, translation included, which is the entire reason 3-D graphics works in $4\times4$ homogeneous coordinates. Every matrix is such a map, with $\ker T = \mathbf{N}(A)$ and range $= \mathbf{C}(A)$, so Week 4 introduces **no new computation at all** — only the knowledge of which Weeks 0–3 computation a question is asking for. The productive theorem is that **a linear map is completely determined by its values on a basis**, chosen arbitrarily: spanning gives uniqueness, independence gives existence. Hence the construction — **column $j$ of the matrix is $T$ applied to basis vector $j$** — and hence that composition is matrix multiplication, which is the theorem Week 1's L04 §1 *defined* multiplication to make true. Differentiation on $\mathbb{P}_3$ becomes a $4\times4$ matrix with $D^4 = 0$, whose kernel is the constants and therefore whose failure to be invertible is the $+\,C$ on every antiderivative. The week's real content is that **a matrix is a transformation plus a basis**: the same reflection is $\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$ in the standard basis and $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ in the basis along and across the mirror, and the individual entries are facts about the choice rather than about the map. What survives a change of basis — trace, determinant, rank — is the real content; $B = M^{-1}AM$ is the conversion, **$M$ takes new coordinates to old**, and the reversal is undetectable by any similarity invariant because $MAM^{-1}$ is similar to $A$ too. **The freedom to choose the basis is the programme for the rest of the course**: Weeks 7, 10 and 11 are the same question with hypotheses that weaken from *sometimes* to *always*.
