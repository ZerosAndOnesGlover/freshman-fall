# MATH 241 · Week 2 · Summary

**Topic —** Vector spaces and subspaces; the column space and the null space, and what each says about solving $Ax = b$.
**Lectures —** L07 Vector Spaces and Subspaces; L08 The Column Space, and When $Ax = b$ Is Solvable; L09 The Null Space, and the Complete Solution.
**Work —** PS 2 (100, released Wednesday, due Friday of Week 3), Quiz 2 (Monday, covers Week 1, unmarked). **PS 1 due Friday.** Recitation 1 is sat Thursday and covers Week 1; Recitation 2 covers this week and is sat Thursday of Week 3.
**Takeaway —** A vector space is a set you can add in and scale in and nothing more — no length, no angle, no product — and that poverty is what makes the theorems transfer to matrices, polynomials and continuous functions alike. A subspace is checked with three conditions, of which $0 \in W$ disposes of most non-examples in one substitution, and the subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^3$ are exactly four: $\{0\}$, a line, a plane, $\mathbb{R}^3$, all through the origin. Two subspaces come out of every matrix and they answer the two halves of Week 0's question: $\mathbf{C}(A) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$ is the span of the columns and says **which $b$** are reachable, while $\mathbf{N}(A) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ says **how many** $x$ reach each one. **They live in different spaces and the commonest error on the problem set is putting them in the same one.** The week's real result is that elimination treats them oppositely: $\mathbf{N}(A) = \mathbf{N}(\operatorname{rref}A)$ **exactly**, because every row operation is an invertible matrix acting on the left, while $\mathbf{C}(A) \ne \mathbf{C}(\operatorname{rref}A)$ — for the running $3\times4$ matrix those are the planes $5b_1 - 2b_2 + b_3 = 0$ and $b_3 = 0$, and $(1,0,0)$ separates them. A row operation mixes entries within each column, so it moves every column and the space they span; it never touches $x$, and the null space is the complete list of relations among the columns, which is what elimination was built to expose. One special solution per free column gives $\operatorname{rank} + \operatorname{nullity} = n$ by counting, and the complete solution is $x_p + \mathbf{N}(A)$ — proved in both directions, with $x_p$ not unique and the set that is.
