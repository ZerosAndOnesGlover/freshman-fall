# PROG 201 · Week 2 · Summary

**Topic —** Pipes, FIFOs and IPC: pipe capacity and atomicity, named pipes, POSIX message queues, shared memory, semaphores, and which to reach for.
**Lectures —** L07 Pipes as a Ring of Pages, and FIFOs; L08 POSIX Message Queues and Shared Memory; L09 Semaphores, and Choosing a Mechanism.
**Work —** PS 2 (100, due Friday of Week 3), Quiz 2 (Tuesday, covers Week 1, unmarked). **Lab 1 is sat on Monday of this week; Lab 2 covers this week and is sat on Monday of Week 3.**
**Takeaway —** A pipe is **sixteen page-sized buffers**, not 65,536 bytes: 4,096-byte writes fill it completely, 4,097-byte writes fill it to 68.8%. `PIPE_BUF` is 4,096 and it bounds your *request*: at or below it four concurrent writers tore **0 of 800** records, above it they tore **792 of 800 — but only when the reader drained in pieces smaller than a record**, and 0 of 8,000 otherwise, which is how an 8 KB record passes every test you will write. A FIFO is a pipe with a name: `open` is a rendezvous, `O_WRONLY|O_NONBLOCK` fails with `ENXIO` where `O_RDONLY|O_NONBLOCK` succeeds, and a server must hold a write end open or the first client's exit ends it. A POSIX message queue gives boundaries and priorities and caps an unprivileged process at **ten messages of 8,192 bytes**; its objects, like every POSIX IPC object, outlive the process. Shared memory is `shm_open`+`ftruncate`+`mmap`, and skipping the `ftruncate` gives **`SIGBUS`, not `SIGSEGV`**. An unguarded shared counter lost **64.7% of 800,000 increments**; a semaphore round it lost none, at 19.3 ns uncontended and ~3.3 µs when it has to sleep. And the textbook claim is wrong as usually stated: a pipe moved bulk data **6× faster** than a one-slot shared-memory ring and lost the round trip by only 8%, because what shared memory removes is the 118 ns copy and not the 3 µs wakeup — `strace` counts **131,114 system calls for the pipe against 131,817 for the ring**, the same number. Deepening the ring to four slots is a **7× gain**; the real variable is message size, where shm runs **0.11× at 64 B, 2.3× at 64 KB and 5.8× at 256 KB**.
