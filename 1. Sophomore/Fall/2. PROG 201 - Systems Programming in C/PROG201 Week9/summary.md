# PROG 201 · Week 9 · Summary

**Topic —** Performance: profiling, the memory hierarchy, the roofline model, compiler optimisation, and benchmarking honestly.
**Lectures —** L28 Measure First, and What a Profiler Costs; L29 Caches, Bandwidth, and the Roofline; L30 Optimising: What Works, What the Compiler Does, and How Not to Fool Yourself.
**Work —** **Project 1 due Friday 17:00 (12.5%)** and **PS 8 due the same day**; PS 9 released Wednesday, due Friday of Week 10; Quiz 9 (Tuesday, covers Week 8, unmarked). **Lab 8 is sat on Monday of this week; Lab 9 covers this week and is sat on the Monday of Week 10.**
**Takeaway —** A program taking 2.35 s got **19% faster from every optimisation flag GCC has** and **60× faster from three source changes** found by profiling — and once the algorithm was right, the flags stopped mattering (0.05 s to 0.04 s across the whole range). `perf` is unusable on these machines (`perf_event_paranoid` is 4), so the week uses **Callgrind** — which named `__strcmp_avx2` as **61.59%** of the program — and a **sampling profiler you write in sixty lines**: `setitimer(ITIMER_PROF)`, a `SA_SIGINFO` handler, and `REG_RIP` out of the `ucontext`. It cannot name static functions without `-rdynamic` (1 dynamic symbol against 14) and cannot name libc's IFUNC internals at all. The hierarchy, measured by pointer chase: **L1 1.49 ns, L2 3.13, L3 11.84, DRAM 141.94 — a factor of 95**, with the steps on the documented cache sizes; a stride table shows the 64-byte line (1.09 ns per element at stride 1, 6.33 at stride 16) and **flattens at 128 bytes, where the prefetcher gives up**. The two ceilings are **13.71 GB/s and 13.50 GFLOP/s**, a **ridge point of 0.98 flops/byte** — below which nothing can be other than memory-bound. `axpy` reached **99% of its roof and is finished**; `poly` reached **18%**, and two kernels with identical arithmetic intensity differed by **2.9×** purely from dependency chains, which the roofline cannot model. Ranked by measured win: algorithm 60×, layout 5.8×, ILP 2.9×, vectorisation 4.8×, flags 1.19×, PGO 1.00×. **`-O3` was slower than `-O2`** on the unfixed program, and **`-O2` does not vectorise while `-O3` does** — 0 SIMD instructions against 10, worth 1.74× out of cache and 4.8× in L1, with the `-O3` version landing exactly on the measured memory ceiling. And the week's rule, needed five times this term: **a number that is impossible is a fact about your measurement** — including this lecture's own first bandwidth figure, which read 6.28 GB/s because it was written as a dependency chain.
