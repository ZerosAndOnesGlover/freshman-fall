# PROG 201 · Week 3 · Summary

**Topic —** POSIX threads: what threads share, mutexes and condition variables, reader-writer locks, priority inversion, and thread pools.
**Lectures —** L10 Threads: What They Share, and What They Cost; L11 Mutexes, Condition Variables, and Reader-Writer Locks; L12 Priority Inversion, Thread Pools, and Choosing.
**Work —** PS 3 (100, due Friday of Week 4), Quiz 3 (Tuesday, covers Week 2, unmarked). **Lab 2 is sat on Monday of this week; Lab 3 covers this week and is sat on Monday of Week 4** — the same afternoon as **Midterm 1** (18:00–19:30, Weeks 0–3).
**Takeaway —** A thread shares the address space, the descriptor table and the PID, and keeps its own stack, registers, `errno` and signal mask; `pthread_create`+`join` costs **29.21 µs** against `fork`+`wait`'s **163.64 µs**, and shrinking the 8 MiB default stack buys no more threads because the ceiling here is the cgroup's `pids.max` of **7,671**. Four threads incrementing one counter lost **70.7% of 800,000** increments — Week 2's result with a different flow of control — and the broken version was **88× faster** than the correct one, which is why the bug survives review. Uncontended, a mutex costs **8.2 ns** and no system call, an rwlock read costs **18.6 ns**, a semaphore **19.2 ns** and an atomic add **5.4 ns**; a reader-writer lock is **0.52× a mutex** with a trivial critical section and **4.15×** with a long one. `pthread_cond_wait` atomically releases the mutex and blocks, which is Week 0's `pause()` race solved again — and the loop must be a `while` because **7.12% of wakeups arrived with nothing to do**. One condition variable serving two predicates with `signal` **deadlocks**, measured; `broadcast` hides it, and glibc requeues rather than stampeding. Priority inversion turned a **0.49 s critical section into a 100.6 s wait**, exactly what the CFS weights predict — and `PTHREAD_PRIO_INHERIT` was accepted, read back correctly, and did **nothing**, because `SCHED_OTHER` threads have no realtime priority to inherit and `RLIMIT_RTPRIO` is 0. What works: equal priorities (1.94 s) and short critical sections (~0.5 s). A thread pool beat thread-per-task by **45.6×** on empty tasks and **7.3×** on expensive ones, peaked at **4 workers** on a 4-core machine and was 42% worse at 8, and with trivial tasks got slower with every worker added.
