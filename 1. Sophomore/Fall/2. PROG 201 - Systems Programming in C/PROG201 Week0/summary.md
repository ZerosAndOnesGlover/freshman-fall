# PROG 201 · Week 0 · Summary

**Topic —** The Unix process model: what a process is, `fork`/`exec`/`wait`, zombies and orphans, and signals as interruptions rather than messages.
**Lectures —** L01 What a Process Is, and What `fork()` Copies; L02 Waiting: Zombies, Orphans, and the Process Table; L03 Signals: `sigaction`, Masks, and Async-Signal-Safe Code.
**Work —** PS 0 (100, due Friday of Week 1), Lab 0 (unmarked, checked off in session, Friday of Week 0). **No quiz** — Quiz 1 in Week 1 covers this week.
**Takeaway —** A process is kernel state, not a program, and `exec` proves it by swapping the program without changing the PID. `fork` copies page tables rather than pages: measured at 34 µs per megabyte of parent, so `posix_spawn` is 70× faster for a 1 GB parent that only wants to run `/bin/true`, and copy-on-write costs a reader 0 minor faults and a writer exactly one per page. A signal is one bit that means *at least once*: 5,000 `SIGUSR1` sent while blocked ran the handler **once**, and real-time signals queue only up to `RLIMIT_SIGPENDING` — 25,571 here — after which `kill()` returns 0 and the signal is discarded anyway. Two measurements contradict the textbook usefully: orphans are adopted by `systemd --user` at PID 257510, not by `init`, and `printf` in a handler corrupted two thirds of its own output while `malloc` in a handler survived 1.49 billion allocations without the deadlock it is guaranteed to be capable of.
