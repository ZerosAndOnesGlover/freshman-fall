**Takeaway —** You understand the specialised machines because you understand the general one. The frontier moves along a single axis — generality against efficiency — and every machine on it answers Week 0's question: when a general core stops getting faster, build a specific machine for the workload and pay for its efficiency in flexibility. RISC-V trades `add eax,[rdi]` (one CISC instruction, verified) for `lw`+`add` and bets simplicity plus openness wins; a TPU discards the caches and branch predictors of Weeks 4–5 because neural nets are regular branchless matmuls, reaching a cited 15–30× perf/watt not by cleverness but by spending nothing on generality. L38 traces every week of the course through one ordinary web-server function, reveals the abstraction hierarchy as the syllabus, and offers the latency ladder — nine orders of magnitude, L1 to an internet round trip — as the one artefact to keep, because its ratios outlast any hardware. The reflex the course really built: when you want to know what the machine does, you look; you do not guess.
