**Q1 (2 pts).** $a=\frac{F}{m}=\frac{30}{6.0}=5.0\ \text{m/s}^2$. $v=v_0+at=0+5.0\times4.0=\boxed{20\ \text{m/s}}$.

*Marks: 2 / 2*

---

**Q2 (2 pts).** A Third Law pair is the same **type** of force with the two objects swapped. "The Moon pulls the Earth gravitationally" is gravity on Earth due to the Moon — the exact partner of gravity on the Moon due to Earth.

**Answer: (A) The Moon pulls the Earth gravitationally**

*Marks: 2 / 2*

---

**Q3 (3 pts).** $N=mg=12\times9.81=117.7$ N; $f_k=\mu_kN=0.30\times117.7=35.3$ N.
$\Sigma F_x=60-35.3=24.7$ N $=12a\Rightarrow a=\boxed{2.06\ \text{m/s}^2}$.

*Marks: 3 / 3*

---

**Q4 (2 pts).** Frictionless incline: only the weight component along the slope accelerates the block, $a=g\sin\theta$.

**Answer: (C) $9.81\sin40^\circ$ m/s²**

*Marks: 2 / 2*

---

**Q5 (3 pts).** $mg=5.0\times9.81=49.05$ N. $T=60$ N $>mg$, so the elevator accelerates **upward**.
$\Sigma F=T-mg=ma\Rightarrow a=\frac{60-49.05}{5.0}=\boxed{2.19\ \text{m/s}^2\ \text{upward}}$.

*Marks: 3 / 3*

---

**Q6 (2 pts).** Static friction matches the applied force exactly, up to the ceiling $\mu_sN$; beyond that the object breaks free.

**Answer: (C) Equal to the applied force, up to a maximum of $\mu_sN$**

*Marks: 2 / 2*

---

**Q7 (3 pts).** $m_1=2.0$ kg, $m_2=6.0$ kg.
$a=\dfrac{(m_2-m_1)g}{m_1+m_2}=\dfrac{(6.0-2.0)\times9.81}{8.0}=\dfrac{39.24}{8.0}=\boxed{4.91\ \text{m/s}^2}$.
$T=\dfrac{2m_1m_2g}{m_1+m_2}=\dfrac{2(2.0)(6.0)(9.81)}{8.0}=\dfrac{235.4}{8.0}=\boxed{29.4\ \text{N}}$.

*Marks: 3 / 3*

---

**Q8 (2 pts).** Net driving force $=m_2g=1.0\times9.81=9.81$ N over total mass $5.0$ kg.
$a=\frac{9.81}{5.0}=\boxed{1.96\ \text{m/s}^2}$.

*Marks: 2 / 2*

---

**Q9 (3 pts).** Force pulling the block up the incline $=m_2g=2.0\times9.81=19.6$ N. Component of the block's weight down the incline $=m_1g\sin30^\circ=3.0\times9.81\times0.5=14.7$ N.
$19.6>14.7$ → the **hanging mass pulls the block up** the incline.
Net force $=19.6-14.7=4.9$ N over total mass $5.0$ kg: $\boxed{a=0.98\ \text{m/s}^2}$ (hanging mass down, block up the slope).

*Marks: 3 / 3*

---

**Q10 (3 pts).** Constant velocity ⇒ $a=0$. The force is directed 20° **below** horizontal:
$F_x=70\cos20^\circ=65.8$ N (forward); $F_y=-70\sin20^\circ=-23.9$ N (downward, adding to the weight).
$\Sigma F_y=0:\ N=mg+F\sin20^\circ=20\times9.81+23.9=220.1$ N.
$\Sigma F_x=0:\ f_k=F\cos20^\circ=65.8$ N.
$\mu_k=\frac{f_k}{N}=\frac{65.8}{220.1}=\boxed{0.30}$.

*Marks: 3 / 3*
