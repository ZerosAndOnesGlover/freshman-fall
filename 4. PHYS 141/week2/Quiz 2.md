**Q1 (2 pts).** Ball rolls off a horizontal table at 3.0 m/s, height 1.2 m. Horizontal velocity remains constant (no horizontal forces); vertical velocity changes. Time to fall depends only on height: $t=\sqrt{2h/g}$ independent of horizontal speed.

**Answer: (C) The time to hit the floor depends only on the height, not the horizontal speed**

*Marks: 2 / 2*

---

**Q2 (2 pts).** $R\propto\sin2\theta$. $\theta=45°$: $\sin90°=1$. $\theta=30°$: $\sin60°\approx0.866$. $H\propto\sin^2\theta$. $\theta=45°$: 0.5; $\theta=30°$: 0.25. So 30° gives smaller range and smaller max height.

**Answer: (C) Smaller range and smaller max height**

*Marks: 2 / 2*

---

**Q3 (3 pts).** $v_0=18$ m/s at $40°$, $v_{0y}=18\sin40°=11.57$ m/s. $H=\frac{v_{0y}^2}{2g}=\frac{133.9}{19.62}=\boxed{6.82\ \text{m}}$.

*Marks: 3 / 3*

---

**Q4 (2 pts).** At the peak of projectile motion, vertical velocity is zero; horizontal velocity remains $v_{0x}=v_0\cos\theta$. Magnitude is $v_{0x}$ (not zero), direction horizontal.

**Answer: (C) The vertical component of velocity is zero; the horizontal component remains constant**

*Marks: 2 / 2*

---

**Q5 (3 pts).** Horizontal: $x=v_0t \implies t=x/v_x=36/12=3.0$ s. Vertical fall from rest (thrown horizontally): $h=\frac{1}{2}gt^2=\frac{1}{2}(9.81)(9.0)=\boxed{44.1\ \text{m}}$.

*Marks: 3 / 3*

---

**Q6 (2 pts).** Uniform circular motion: speed constant, direction changes continuously → velocity changes → acceleration non-zero. Centripetal acceleration is directed toward center (perpendicular to velocity). Magnitude constant; direction rotates.

**Answer: (C) The speed is constant but the velocity is changing**

*Marks: 2 / 2*

---

**Q7 (3 pts).** Angular speed $\omega=6.0$ rad/s, $r=0.50$ m. Centripetal acceleration $a_c=\omega^2 r=(36)(0.50)=\boxed{18\ \text{m/s}^2}$ toward center.

*Marks: 3 / 3*

---

**Q8 (2 pts).** $a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}$. If $v$ doubles and $r$ same, $v^2$ becomes 4× → $a_c$ becomes 4×.

**Answer: (B) It becomes four times larger**

*Marks: 2 / 2*

---

**Q9 (3 pts).** Car speed $v=25$ m/s, $r=200$ m. $a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}=\frac{625}{200}=\boxed{3.13\ \text{m/s}^2}$ toward center. Tangential acceleration $a_t=\frac{d|v|}{dt}=1.5$ m/s² along velocity. Total magnitude $a=\sqrt{a_c^2+a_t^2}=\sqrt{9.77+2.25}=\sqrt{12.02}=\boxed{3.47\ \text{m/s}^2}$.

*Marks: 3 / 3*

---

**Q10 (3 pts).** $R=\frac{v_0^2\sin2\theta}{g}$, $H=\frac{v_0^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}$. Set $R=4H$:

$$\frac{v_0^2}{g}2\sin\theta\cos\theta = 4\cdot\frac{v_0^2}{2g}\sin^2\theta = 2\frac{v_0^2}{g}\sin^2\theta.$$

Thus $2\sin\theta\cos\theta = 2\sin^2\theta \implies \cos\theta=\sin\theta \implies \tan\theta=1 \implies \theta=\boxed{45^\circ}$.

*Marks: 3 / 3*
