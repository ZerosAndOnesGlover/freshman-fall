## Part A — Discs and Washers (5 pts each)

**A1.** The region under $y=\sqrt x$ on $[0,4]$, rotated about the $x$-axis.

**A2.** The region between $y=2x$ and $y=x^2$, rotated about the $x$-axis.

*Find the intersection points first. Decide which curve is outer by evaluating both somewhere inside.*

**A3.** The region under $y=\dfrac1x$ on $[1,3]$, rotated about the $x$-axis.

**A4.** The region under $y=x^2$ on $[0,1]$, rotated about the line $\boldsymbol{y=2}$.

*Not the $x$-axis. Draw it, and note there is a hole.*

## Part C — Arc Length and Surface Area (6 pts each)

**C1.** Find the arc length of $y=\tfrac23x^{3/2}$ on $[0,3]$.

**C2.** Find the arc length of $y=\ln(\cos x)$ on $\left[0,\tfrac\pi4\right]$.

*Compute $1+(y')^2$ and simplify with a trigonometric identity before integrating. The integral is one you met in Week 1.*

**C3.** Find the area of the surface generated by rotating $y=x^3$ on $[0,1]$ about the $x$-axis.

**C4.** Find the area of the surface generated by rotating $y=2\sqrt x$ on $[1,2]$ about the $x$-axis.

*Combine the radicals before integrating.*

**C5.** *(Set up, then decide.)* Consider the arc length of $y=e^x$ on $[0,1]$.

- (a) Write down the integral.
- (b) It looks intractable. **Determine whether it can in fact be evaluated in closed form**, and if so evaluate it. *(Try the substitution $u=e^x$ and see what kind of integral results.)*
- (c) Compare with the arc length of $y=\sin x$ on $[0,\pi]$, which **cannot** be evaluated in closed form. What is structurally different about the two?

## Part D — Concept (10 pts each)

**D1.** *(One pattern, four formulas.)* Week 0 gave the pattern **slice — approximate — sum — take the limit**.

For each of the four quantities below, state (i) how the region or curve is sliced, (ii) what simple shape each piece is approximated by, and (iii) the resulting formula. Derive each in two or three lines; do not merely quote it.

- (a) Volume by discs
- (b) Volume by cylindrical shells
- (c) Arc length
- (d) Surface area of revolution

Then answer: **in (d), why is the width of each band $ds$ rather than $dx$?** What goes wrong if you use $dx$?

**D2.** *(Two methods, one solid.)* The region under $y=x^2$ on $[0,2]$ is rotated about the $y$-axis.

- (a) Compute the volume **by shells**.
- (b) Compute the same volume **by discs**, slicing horizontally. You will need to express the boundary as a function of $y$ and treat the solid as a washer against $x=2$.
- (c) Confirm the two agree.
- (d) Now consider rotating the region under $y=\sin x$ on $[0,\pi]$ about the $y$-axis. Explain why the shell method is straightforward here but the disc method is substantially harder — and be specific about what property of $\sin x$ on $[0,\pi]$ causes the difficulty.
