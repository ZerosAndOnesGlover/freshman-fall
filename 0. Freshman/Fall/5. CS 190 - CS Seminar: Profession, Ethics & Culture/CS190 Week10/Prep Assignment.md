## Part A — Read a Number Correctly (≈150 words)

Frey and Osborne's 47% is the most-cited statistic in this debate and one of the most-misreported.

1. In **one sentence**, state precisely what the 47% refers to, as the paper defines it.
2. In **one sentence**, state how you have most often heard it reported.
3. In **two or three sentences**, explain what the task-based reanalysis (≈9%) changed and why that
   change produces so different a number.

You are not being asked which figure is correct. **You are being asked to show that you can state a
finding as its authors stated it** — which is the skill the rest of this course has been building.

## Part B — Locate Yourself (≈150 words)

Take the career you currently intend, and decompose it into **four or five concrete tasks** — not
"software engineer" but the actual things that occupy the hours.

For each task, mark it **substitutable**, **complemented**, or **unaffected** by the automation now
visible, and give a one-line reason.

Then answer: **if the substitutable tasks went away, is what remains still your job, a different job,
or no job?**

Be honest rather than reassuring. Students who conclude their intended work is largely automatable
are not marked down; students who conclude it is entirely safe without argument are.

---

## Part C — Commit to a Position (≈100 words)

State your current position on universal basic income in **one sentence**, beginning "I currently
think that…".

Then, in two or three sentences:

- Name **which of the five arguments** from the lecture your position rests on (automation insurance,
  administrative simplicity, bargaining power, real freedom, recognition of unpaid work) — or, if you
  oppose UBI, which objection is doing the work.
- State **what evidence would change your mind.** Be specific enough that someone could go and look
  for it.

If you cannot name evidence that would change your mind, write that down instead. It is an honest
answer and a useful thing to have noticed before Week 11.
