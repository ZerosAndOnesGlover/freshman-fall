# Glossary — Week 6 Additions

---

**LAWS (Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems)**
The term of art, used particularly in UN Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW) discussions, for weapons systems capable of selecting and engaging targets without human intervention. Definitional boundaries are actively contested and central to the policy debate over regulation or prohibition.

**Human-in-the-Loop / Human-on-the-Loop / Human-out-of-the-Loop**
A three-tier framework describing the degree of human involvement in an autonomous system's decision-making. In-the-loop: a human authorizes each individual action. On-the-loop: the system acts autonomously with human monitoring and override capability. Out-of-the-loop: the system acts with no human review of individual decisions, potentially after only general mission-level authorization.

**Accountability Gap**
The problem, central to the ethical and legal debate over autonomous weapons (and, by extension, other autonomous or AI-driven systems causing harm), that existing frameworks for assigning moral and legal responsibility — built around discrete human decisions — do not map cleanly onto harms caused by learned, probabilistic, or distributed systems, leaving no clearly identifiable responsible party for a specific failure.

**Distinction and Proportionality**
Two foundational principles of International Humanitarian Law (IHL) governing the conduct of armed conflict: distinction requires combatants to distinguish between military targets and civilians; proportionality prohibits attacks causing civilian harm excessive relative to the concrete military advantage anticipated. Central to evaluating whether a weapons system (autonomous or otherwise) can be lawfully deployed.

**Deepfake**
Synthetic media (image, audio, or video) generated or manipulated using deep learning techniques (commonly GANs or diffusion models) to convincingly depict a person doing or saying something they did not do or say.

**NCII (Non-Consensual Intimate Imagery)**
Sexually explicit imagery created or distributed without the depicted person's consent. In the deepfake context, the largest category of harmful synthetic media by documented volume, disproportionately targeting women.

**Liar's Dividend**
A concept from legal scholars Bobby Chesney and Danielle Citron describing the second-order harm of deepfake proliferation: as public awareness of synthetic media increases, genuine, authentic evidence of wrongdoing can be dismissed as "probably fake" by the party it implicates, degrading the evidentiary value of all media, not just actual fakes.

**C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity)**
An industry coalition (including Adobe, Microsoft, and others) developing technical standards for cryptographically signing and tracking the provenance of authentic media at the point of capture, as an alternative approach to deepfake detection — verifying authenticity rather than detecting fakery.

**Hallucination (LLM)**
A large language model output that is fluent, confident, and stylistically indistinguishable from accurate content, but factually false — a term describing the phenomenon of models generating plausible-sounding but ungrounded claims, citations, or facts.

**Stochastic Parrot**
A term from Bender, Gebru, McMillan-Major, and Shmitchell's 2021 paper describing large language models as systems that manipulate linguistic form based on statistical patterns in training data without access to or understanding of meaning — a contested characterization actively debated in light of subsequent model capabilities.

**Task Automation vs. Job Elimination**
An analytical distinction between a technology automating specific tasks within a job (changing what the job involves) and eliminating the job category entirely (removing the need for the role). Frequently conflated in public discussion of AI's labor market effects; the actual balance between the two is occupation-specific and empirically contested.

**Existential Risk (AI)**
A category of long-term risk concern, associated with some AI safety researchers, that sufficiently advanced AI systems could pose risks to humanity's long-term survival or flourishing qualitatively distinct from nearer-term AI harms (bias, misinformation, labor disruption). A genuinely contested area within the AI research community, with serious disagreement on plausibility, timeline, and mechanism.
