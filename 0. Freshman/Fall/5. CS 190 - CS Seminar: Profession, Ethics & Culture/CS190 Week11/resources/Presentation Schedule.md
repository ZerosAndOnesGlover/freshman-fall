# CS 190 · Week 11
## Presentation Schedule

**Every student presents this week.** Five minutes presenting, three minutes questions, eight minutes
per slot.

---

## Why Two Sessions

The seminar is scheduled for one hour, which accommodates seven slots including transitions. A section
of twenty-four students therefore requires **two additional sessions** beyond the regular meeting.

| Session | When | Slots |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Session A** | Wednesday 9 December 2026, 13:00–13:50 (regular seminar slot) | 1–7 |
| **Session B** | Week 11, additional session (see course page) | 8–15 |
| **Session C** | Week 11, additional session (see course page) | 16–24 |

**Attendance at all three sessions is required, not only the one in which you present.** Audience
participation is assessed — see [[Peer Feedback Form]] — and a presenter speaking to an empty room
has been denied the thing that makes the exercise worth doing.

---

## Running Order

Slots are assigned **by lot**, drawn in the Week 10 seminar, and posted on the course page. Two
consequences:

- **Nobody chooses to go last.** The order is not a judgement, a reward, or a punishment.
- **Order is not thematic.** Consecutive presentations will jump between unrelated topics, which is
  normal and is not a scheduling error.

### Swaps

You may swap slots with another student by **mutual agreement**, provided both of you email the
instructor at least **24 hours** before the earlier of the two slots. Swaps are between students; the
instructor records rather than arbitrates them.

---

## Session Structure

Each session runs as follows:

| Time | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:03 | Instructor opens; ground rules restated |
| 0:03–… | Presentations, eight minutes each, back to back |
| Final 5 min | Closing round — one thing you heard that changed your mind |

**Transitions are 30 seconds.** Set up while the previous presenter takes questions. If you are using
slides, they must already be on the shared machine before the session starts; **connecting a personal
laptop mid-session is not permitted**, because it reliably costs two minutes that belong to somebody
else.

---

## Timing Enforcement

- A card is raised at **4:00** — one minute remaining.
- At **5:00** you are stopped, mid-sentence if necessary.
- Questions run to **3:00** and then close, whether or not hands remain up.

Every person in the room has the same five minutes. Running over does not take time from the
instructor; it takes time from the student who follows you.

---

## If You Cannot Present at Your Slot

**Email the instructor before the session, not after.** Illness, religious observance, an
accessibility requirement, and a genuine clash are all handled the same way: a slot is found in one
of the other two sessions, at no penalty and with no explanation required beyond the fact of the
conflict.

If you miss all three sessions for a documented reason, a slot is arranged in Week 12 or in office
hours. **A recorded presentation is accepted only where an accessibility requirement makes live
presentation unreasonable**, and is marked against the same rubric except for criterion E
(questions), which is redistributed across A–C.

**Silently not appearing** is the one case that is penalised, because it cannot be distinguished from
non-completion, and because the slot went unused while somebody else could have taken it.

---

## Accessibility

Anything you need — presenting seated, extended time, a quieter room, materials in advance, a
different format — is arranged by emailing the instructor, and is arranged without requiring you to
disclose a reason. This is stated here rather than buried in a handbook because **Week 9 discussed at
some length the cost of environments where people do not ask.**
