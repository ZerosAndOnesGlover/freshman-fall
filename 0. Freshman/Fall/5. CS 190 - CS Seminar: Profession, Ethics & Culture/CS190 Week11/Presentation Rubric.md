# CS 190 — Week 11
## Presentation Rubric

**Total: 20 points.** Marked into the **Participation** component (40% of the course grade). Marks
are returned within one week, with written comments on the two lowest-scoring criteria.

---

## The Marking Scheme

| Criterion | Points | What is being judged |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. A claim was made** | 5 | A position was stated clearly, early, and in a form someone could disagree with |
| **B. A reason was given** | 5 | One line of argument, developed rather than gestured at |
| **C. The objection was real** | 5 | The strongest opposing view was stated fairly, and responded to honestly |
| **D. Delivery and timing** | 3 | Audible, not read from a script, finished within five minutes |
| **E. Questions** | 2 | Answered what was asked; conceded what could not be answered |
| **Total** | **20** | |

**Fourteen of the twenty points are for the argument.** This is deliberate. A nervous, halting
delivery of a real argument scores well above a fluent delivery of a topic summary.

---

## A. A Claim Was Made — 5 points

| Score | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **5** | Position stated in the first 45 seconds, in one or two sentences, and specific enough that a reasonable person could hold the opposite |
| **4** | Clear position, but stated late or requiring the audience to assemble it from several remarks |
| **3** | A position is present but hedged to the point where its content is hard to pin down |
| **2** | Mostly topic description; a position is implied but never asserted |
| **0–1** | No position. The presentation reported on a subject |

> **The commonest way to lose points here** is ending on "so it's complicated." It is complicated;
> that is why you were asked for a position rather than a summary.

## B. A Reason Was Given — 5 points

| Score | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **5** | One line of argument, developed properly, with the inference from reason to claim visible |
| **4** | Sound reasoning, but a step is left implicit that the audience must supply |
| **3** | Two or more lines of argument, each half-developed — nothing actually established |
| **2** | Reason offered is an assertion of the claim in different words |
| **0–1** | No reason given, or reason unrelated to the claim |

> **Evidence is credited where it is used**, not where it is cited. Naming a source without stating
> what it shows earns nothing.

## C. The Objection Was Real — 5 points

| Score | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **5** | The genuinely hardest objection, stated as its proponent would state it, with a response that names what it costs |
| **4** | A serious objection fairly stated, with a response that slightly overclaims |
| **3** | A real objection, but stated in a weakened form that makes it easier to answer |
| **2** | A token objection chosen because it was easy to dismiss |
| **0–1** | No objection engaged, or the objection is a straw man |

> **This is the criterion that separates presentations.** Conceding that part of an objection stands
> scores **higher** than claiming to have answered all of it. Pretending an objection was easy is the
> clearest signal that the presenter has not yet understood it.

## D. Delivery and Timing — 3 points

| Score | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **3** | Audible throughout, spoken from notes rather than read, finished within time with the argument complete |
| **2** | Minor issues — occasional inaudibility, some passages read verbatim, or slightly rushed at the end |
| **1** | Substantially read aloud from a script, or cut off at 5:00 with the argument incomplete |
| **0** | Inaudible, or did not present |

> Nervousness is **not** penalised. Pauses, restarts and visible nerves cost nothing. Reading
> continuous prose aloud does.

## E. Questions — 2 points

| Score | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **2** | Answered what was actually asked; conceded openly where the answer was not available |
| **1** | Answered adjacent questions rather than the ones asked, or over-defended |
| **0** | Refused engagement, or invented an answer |

> **"I don't know" is marked neutrally, not penalised.** Inventing an answer is penalised. This is
> the same standard the course has applied to disclosure, to evidence, and to prediction: say what
> you can support, and mark the boundary where support runs out.

---

## Automatic Considerations

- **Not presenting** scores 0 and, per the brief, means the participation component cannot be passed.
  Contact the instructor **before** the session if you cannot present; see `Presentation Schedule.md`.
- **Overrunning** is handled by the timing rule, not by a separate penalty. You are stopped at 5:00
  and marked on what was delivered.
- **Presenting a position you have since abandoned** is fine and costs nothing, provided you say so.

---

## A Note on What This Rubric Is For

The scheme rewards the things that are hard and cheap to fake: stating a position, engaging the
strongest objection, admitting what you cannot answer. It gives almost no credit for polish, slides,
or confidence.

That ordering is the point of the course. **In professional practice you will be believed partly
because you present well, and that is exactly why you should be able to tell the difference between
presenting well and being right.**
