# CS 190 — Week 11
## Peer Feedback Form

**Complete one form for each presentation you attend.** Forms are collected at the end of each
session and are part of the **Participation** component.

> **These forms do not affect the presenter's mark.** The rubric is applied by the instructor. Your
> feedback is returned to the presenter as feedback, and is marked as *your* work — on whether you
> listened to an argument well enough to describe it.

---

## The Form

Copy this block once per presentation. Handwriting is fine.

```
Presenter: ______________________     Your name: ______________________

1. THEIR CLAIM, IN YOUR WORDS (one sentence — do not use their sentence)



2. THE REASON THEY GAVE FOR IT (one sentence)



3. THE OBJECTION THEY ENGAGED — and was it the strongest one available?
   ( ) Yes, it was the hardest objection I can think of
   ( ) Partly — a stronger one exists, namely:
   ( ) No — a much stronger objection is:



4. ONE THING THAT WORKED



5. ONE THING THAT WOULD HAVE MADE THE ARGUMENT STRONGER
   (about the argument, not the delivery — "speak up" is not useful feedback here)



6. DID THIS CHANGE YOUR MIND ABOUT ANYTHING?   ( ) Yes  ( ) No  ( ) Partly
   If yes or partly, what:
```

---

## How Your Forms Are Marked

Not on generosity, and not on severity. On **whether you understood the argument.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Strong** | Question 1 restates the claim accurately in genuinely different words; question 5 identifies something about the argument's structure |
| **Adequate** | The claim is recovered roughly; question 5 is about content but general |
| **Weak** | Question 1 reproduces the presenter's phrasing or names the topic instead of the claim; question 5 is about delivery |

**Question 1 is the whole test.** If you can restate someone's position in your own words, accurately
enough that they would accept the restatement, you were listening. If you can only name their topic,
you were not — and the ability to state an opponent's view in a form they would accept is a
prerequisite for disagreeing with it usefully.

## Your Obligations as Audience

**Ask at least three questions across the three sessions.** Not three per presentation — three in
total, spread across the week. Track them yourself; you will report them in the Week 12 reflection.

A useful question:
- is **short** — under fifteen seconds;
- is about **the argument**, not the topic ("why does your reason support that claim?" beats "what do
  you think about X?");
- is **not a speech with a question mark at the end**, which is the most common failure and is
  usually the questioner presenting their own paper uninvited.

## Ground Rules

The rule from Week 9 applies to this week in full:

> **Arguments are about evidence and reasoning. No argument may require anyone present to defend
> their own existence in the room as a debating position.**

Some of these papers will be about diversity, about mental health, about work people in the room are
doing under conditions they did not choose. Presenters may be arguing from experience. **Disagree
with the argument, which is what the presenter has offered for disagreement.**

Two further rules, both from the same principle:

**Do not out anyone by inference.** If a presenter's argument implies something about their own
circumstances that they did not state, do not name it in a question.

**A presentation is not a referendum on the presenter.** You are marking arguments this week, and the
strongest thing you can do for a classmate whose position you think is wrong is to state the best
objection to it clearly and let them answer.

---

## Why Peer Feedback Is Assessed at All

Because it is the harder half of the exercise and the half that gets skipped.

Five minutes of presenting is a small part of a professional life. **Sitting through someone else's
argument and reconstructing it faithfully before disagreeing** is most of one — in design review, in
incident postmortems, in every disclosure decision this course has examined. The form drills the
reconstruction, which is the part people are worst at.
