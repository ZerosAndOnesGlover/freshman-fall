# CS 190 · CS Seminar: Profession, Ethics & Culture
## Week 0: What is Computer Science? Careers in CS, Software, and Hardware Engineering

**Format:** Weekly 1-hour seminar + readings
**Assessment for this course (overall):** Participation 40%, Position Papers 60%
**This week's deliverable:** Discussion preparation notes (participation-graded, not a formal position paper — those begin Week 3)

---

### Why This Week Exists

CS 190 is the only course in your Year 1 schedule that asks a different kind of question than your technical courses. CS 101, PROG 101, MATH 141, and MATH 151 all ask: *how do I solve this problem correctly?* CS 190 asks: *what am I actually becoming, and what will I be responsible for?*

Most students arrive at a CS/CSE program with a blurry picture of what "computer science" even means as a discipline, how it differs from software engineering and computer engineering, and what kind of career terrain they're walking into. Week 0 exists to make that picture sharp before you spend four years inside the discipline without ever stepping back to see its shape.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of Week 0, you should be able to:

1. Articulate the distinction between Computer Science, Software Engineering, and Computer Engineering as fields — not just as job titles, but as different intellectual traditions with different core questions.
2. Describe at least three major career paths available to a CSE graduate and the skill profile each one rewards.
3. Explain why a technical discipline needs an ethics seminar at all — i.e., make the case for why CS 190 exists.
4. Identify your own current assumptions about "what computer scientists do" and be ready to test them against the readings and discussion.

### How This Course Is Graded (Reminder)

- **Participation (40%):** Weekly seminar attendance and engagement. Quality over quantity — one sharp, well-reasoned comment beats five reflexive ones. Participation is graded weekly starting Week 0.
- **Position Papers (60%):** Beginning Week 3, you will write short (750–1000 word) position papers on assigned ethical questions. These are argumentative, not summary — you must take and defend a stance.

### A Note on Tone

This seminar will sometimes ask you to defend positions you don't fully believe, or to sit with discomfort about a technology you're excited to build. That's the point. An engineer who has only ever practiced their own opinions hasn't actually practiced ethical reasoning, they've practiced confirmation. Come ready to be unsettled a little.
