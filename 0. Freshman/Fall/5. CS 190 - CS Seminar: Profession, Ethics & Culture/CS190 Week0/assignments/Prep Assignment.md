# Prep Assignment: Week 0 (Participation-Graded)

**Due:** Wednesday 23 September 2026, 12:00 — before the 13:00 seminar.
**Read first:** this week's lecture notes (`Lecture Week 0.md`) and the Reading Guide. The seminar discusses the
lecture; it does not introduce it, so the notes are part of the preparation.
**Grading:** Participation component (40% of course grade is participation; this is one weekly instance of it). Not a formal position paper, those start Week 3. Graded credit/no-credit on completion and evident effort, not on "correctness" (there isn't one).

---

## What to Submit / Bring

A single page (handwritten or typed, your choice. This is a thinking tool, not a polished deliverable) containing:

### Part A: Reading Notes (the bulk of the page)

For **each** of the three required readings (Dijkstra, ACM Computing Curricula excerpt, Russell & Norvig §1.1), answer in 1–2 sentences each:

1. Central claim
2. Strongest supporting evidence/reasoning
3. Strongest counterargument
4. Your own position

(This is the same four-question structure from [[CS190 Week0/resources/Reading Guide|Reading Guide]] — use it directly.)

### Part B: One Discussion Question, Chosen in Advance

From [[CS190 Week0/resources/Discussion Questions|Discussion Questions]], pick **one** question (other than the warm-up questions 1–2) that you find most interesting or most uncomfortable, and write a short paragraph (4–6 sentences) actually attempting an answer. You will likely be asked to share this live, so write something you're willing to say out loud.

### Part C: One Question You Have for the Class

Write one open question — something the readings or the lecture raised for you that wasn't resolved. This can be a clarifying question, a devil's-advocate challenge, or a genuine point of confusion. Seminars run on questions, not just answers; a sharp question is worth as much credit as a sharp answer.

---

## Why This Format

This prep sheet is intentionally low-friction (one page, any format) because the goal is to lower the barrier to showing up *prepared*, not to create another graded essay. The position papers later in the semester will demand polish and argumentative rigor. This week's prep work demands only that you've actually engaged with the material before walking into the room. The seminar discussion is where the real thinking happens; this page just makes sure you arrive ready to do it.
