# Prep Assignment: Week 3 (Participation-Graded)

**Due:** Before seminar begins.
**Format:** One page, any format.
**Grading:** Credit/no-credit.

---

## Part A: Code Mapping

Read the ACM Code before class. Then, for **two** of the three scenarios in the lecture (Scenario A: facial recognition, Scenario B: hospital vulnerability, Scenario C: recommendation algorithm), list the specific ACM principles you believe apply — by section number — and in one sentence each explain why that principle is relevant.

You are not being asked to resolve the scenario. You are being asked to demonstrate you can map real situations onto the code's structure. This is the first step in applying a professional code — identification before analysis.

## Part B: Preparing for Your Position Paper

The Position Paper prompt asks you to take a position on professional obligation when an engineer believes a system poses public risk. Before the seminar, write 3–5 sentences that sketch the position you currently lean toward. This is provisional — you are allowed to change your mind during discussion. But arriving without a provisional view means arriving unprepared.

Your sketch should include: (1) the rough position, (2) the specific ACM principle(s) most relevant to it, (3) one thing you're uncertain about that the seminar might help clarify.

## Part C: One Strong Objection

Write the strongest objection you can construct to the following claim — argue against it as effectively as you can, regardless of your personal view:

*"A computing professional who ships a system they privately believe poses meaningful public risk, without having raised that concern through every available internal channel, has violated their professional obligations under the ACM Code."*

The purpose of this exercise is to practice steelmanning — finding the best case against a position before arguing for or against it. This skill is what distinguishes a position paper from a rant.
