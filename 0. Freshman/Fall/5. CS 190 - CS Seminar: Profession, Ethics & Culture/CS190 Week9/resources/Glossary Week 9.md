# Glossary — Week 9 Additions

---

**Attrition** — Departure from a field or role. Distinguished from recruitment in diversity analysis
because the two have different causes and different remedies; a programme that improves hiring while
ignoring attrition addresses only one end of the problem.

**Burnout** — Classified by the WHO in ICD-11 (QD85) as an **occupational phenomenon**, not a medical
condition, resulting from *chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed*.
Characterised by three dimensions: exhaustion, cynical detachment from the job, and reduced
professional efficacy. **The definition locates the cause in the workplace.**

**Crunch** — A sustained period of mandatory overtime preceding a deadline. Reliably fails to produce
output proportionate to the hours worked, and beyond a few weeks can reduce total output through
elevated defect rates.

**Epistemic argument (for diversity)** — The claim that homogeneous teams share correlated blind spots
that produce products failing for populations the team does not resemble. Instrumentally useful and
empirically contestable. **Distinct from the justice argument** and should not be merged with it.

**Imposter phenomenon** — First described by Clance and Imes (1978): the persistent belief that one is
a fraud despite objective evidence of competence. Commonly called *imposter syndrome*. **The term can
misdiagnose**, by relocating inside an individual's cognition what is sometimes an accurate reading of
a hostile environment.

**Justice argument (for diversity)** — The claim that people should not face worse prospects in a
profession because of characteristics irrelevant to the work. Stands independently of any
productivity benefit and does not become void if the epistemic argument fails empirically.

**Leaky pipeline** — Metaphor for progressive loss of underrepresented people at successive career
stages. Useful but incomplete: it frames the problem as flow through a fixed structure rather than
asking why the structure leaks.

**On-call rotation** — An arrangement for out-of-hours incident response. A genuine operational
requirement; what varies is staffing depth, compensation, protected recovery, and whether alert
volume is treated as a problem to be reduced or a cost to be absorbed by whoever is holding the pager.

**Passion rhetoric** — Framing employment as an expression of personal enthusiasm rather than a
professional relationship. Can be sincere; can also function to extract uncompensated hours and to
mark as insufficiently committed anyone with a life outside the job.

**Sponsorship** — Advocating for someone's advancement using one's own standing — distinct from
**mentoring**, which is advice. Sponsorship spends the sponsor's capital and shows stronger evidence
of affecting advancement.

**Structural harm** — Harm arising from the aggregate of many uncoordinated decisions rather than from
any identifiable choice. Difficult for agent-centred ethical frameworks to handle, since there is no
act to evaluate and no party to hold responsible. The subject of Position Paper #3.

**Structured interview** — An interview using predetermined questions and consistent evaluation
criteria across candidates. Shows better evidence for predictive validity and for reducing disparate
outcomes than unstructured conversation.

**Supererogatory** — In ethics, describing an act that is praiseworthy but not required — going beyond
duty. **The distinction from obligation is the crux of Position Paper #3**: it is easy to argue that
good conduct is admirable, and much harder to argue that failing to perform it is a wrong.
