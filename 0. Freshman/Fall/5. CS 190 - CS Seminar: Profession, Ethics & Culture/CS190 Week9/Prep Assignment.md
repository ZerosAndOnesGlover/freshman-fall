# Prep Assignment — Week 9 (Participation-Graded)

**Due:** Wednesday 25 November 2026, 12:00 — before the 13:00 seminar.
**Read first:** this week's lecture notes (`Lecture Week 9.md`) and the Reading Guide. The seminar discusses the
lecture; it does not introduce it, so the notes are part of the preparation.

**Length:** Roughly one page.

---

## Part A: Account for the Decline

In no more than 150 words, state what you think best explains why computing's share of women degree
recipients **fell** after 1984 while comparable fields rose.

**Then name one thing you would need to observe to know whether you are right.** The second sentence
is the harder half of the assignment.

---

## Part B: Two Arguments, Separated

Write the **justice** argument for diversity in three sentences. Then write the **epistemic** argument
in three sentences.

Now answer, in one sentence each:

- Which argument would survive a study finding no product-quality effect?
- Which argument is easier to make to a sceptical executive, and does that matter?

---

## Part C: Your Own Limit

The lecture claims that people who have not decided their limits in advance discover them only by
passing them.

Write down **one specific commitment** you intend to hold in your first job — something concrete
enough that you would notice yourself breaking it. Examples of the right level of specificity: a
number of consecutive weeks of overtime you would not exceed; a category of request you would decline;
a thing you would say aloud rather than let pass.

Then add **one sentence** on what would make it hard to keep.

**You will not be asked to share this** unless you volunteer it. Write it honestly rather than
impressively.

---

## What "Participation-Graded" Means Here

Assessed on evidence of genuine engagement. Part A rewards the *falsification* sentence more than the
explanation; Part C rewards specificity over ambition.

A student who writes "I would never work unpaid overtime" has written something less useful than one
who writes "I would not accept a third consecutive week of it without raising it with my manager, and
I think the hard part would be that everyone else on the team is doing it."
