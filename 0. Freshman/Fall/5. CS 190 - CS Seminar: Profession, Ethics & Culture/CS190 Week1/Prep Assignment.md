# Prep Assignment — Week 1 (Participation-Graded)

**Due:** Wednesday 30 September 2026, 12:00 — before the 13:00 seminar.
**Read first:** this week's lecture notes (`Lecture Week 1.md`) and the Reading Guide. The seminar discusses the
lecture; it does not introduce it, so the notes are part of the preparation.
**Format:** One page, any format — handwritten or typed.
**Grading:** Credit/no-credit on completion and evident engagement.

---

## Part A: Turing Reading Response

Turing's 1950 paper lists and responds to nine objections against machine intelligence. In your own words:

1. Name two objections you found most interesting and state, in one sentence each, what Turing's response was.
2. Choose one of those two objections and write 3–4 sentences arguing that Turing's response is *insufficient* — that the objection survives his rebuttal. You do not need to personally agree with the objection; argue it as well as you can regardless.

## Part B: Historical Turning Point

From the lecture's timeline, identify the single event or development you consider most consequential for the world as it exists today — not necessarily the most technically impressive, but the one whose absence would have changed the most. Write 3–5 sentences justifying your choice. This will be discussed in the seminar, so come ready to defend it.

## Part C: Lovelace's Claim

Lovelace wrote in 1843: "The Analytical Engine has no power of originating anything. It can only do what we know how to order it to perform."

In exactly 4–6 sentences, state whether you think this claim is (a) still fully true, (b) still true in spirit but technically complicated by modern AI, or (c) no longer true. Commit to a position and give a reason.
