# CS 190 · CS Seminar: Profession, Ethics & Culture
## Week 1: The History of Computing
### From Babbage to Turing to Silicon Valley

**Format:** Weekly 1-hour seminar + readings
**Assessment for this course (overall):** Participation 40%, Position Papers 60%
**This week's deliverable:** Discussion preparation notes (participation-graded)

---

### Why This Week Exists

Every technology you will build arrives with a history attached, and that history is not decoration — it explains why the field's tools, norms, and blind spots look the way they do. Week 1 traces the line from mechanical calculators through the formalisation of logic to the transistor and the personal computer, so that later weeks' arguments about responsibility have somewhere to stand.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of Week 1, you should be able to:

1. Trace the main line of computing history from the mechanical era through the personal computer, identifying what problem each stage was trying to solve.
2. Explain how the formalisation of logic (1850s–1930s) made general-purpose computation conceivable before any machine could perform it.
3. Describe the transistor's significance as an economic event, not merely a technical one.
4. Identify at least one point where the field's history explains a convention you had assumed was arbitrary or inevitable.

### How This Course Is Graded (Reminder)

- **Participation (40%):** Weekly seminar attendance and engagement. Quality over quantity — one sharp, well-reasoned comment beats five reflexive ones.
- **Position Papers (60%):** Three 750–1,000 word argumentative papers, assigned Weeks 3, 6 and 9 (20% each). These are argumentative, not summary — you must take and defend a stance. The word limits are hard, and the papers escalate deliberately: #1 supplies the ACM Code as scaffold, #2 removes it, #3 removes the identifiable agent.
