# Glossary — Week 7 Additions

---

**Idea/Expression Dichotomy**
The foundational copyright principle (17 U.S.C. § 102(b)) that copyright protects original expression but not the underlying ideas, procedures, processes, systems, or methods of operation embodied in that expression. For software: specific source code is protected; the algorithm it implements is not.

**Fair Use**
A US copyright doctrine (17 U.S.C. § 107) permitting certain unlicensed uses of copyrighted work, evaluated through a four-factor balancing test: purpose and character of the use (including whether it is transformative), nature of the copyrighted work, amount and substantiality of the portion used, and effect on the market for the original.

**Google LLC v. Oracle America, Inc. (2021)**
A US Supreme Court decision holding that Google's copying of approximately 11,500 lines of Oracle's Java API declaring code into Android constituted fair use, emphasizing the functional (thinly-protected) nature of API declaring code and the transformative purpose of enabling a new computing platform. The Court assumed, without deciding, that the code was copyrightable at all.

**Patent-Eligible Subject Matter**
The requirement (35 U.S.C. § 101) that a patent claim be directed to a process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, and not merely to an abstract idea, law of nature, or natural phenomenon. The central battleground for software patent litigation.

**Alice/Mayo Framework**
The two-step test established by *Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank International* (2014), building on *Mayo Collaborative Services v. Prometheus Laboratories* (2012), for determining patent-eligible subject matter: (1) is the claim directed to an abstract idea, law of nature, or natural phenomenon? (2) if so, does the claim add an "inventive concept" sufficient to transform it into a patent-eligible application, beyond merely implementing the abstract idea using conventional, generic technology?

**Patent Troll (Non-Practicing Entity)**
An entity that acquires patents not to practice the underlying invention but to extract licensing fees or litigation settlements from operating companies, often through the threat of costly litigation regardless of the underlying claim's merits. A significant critique specific to the software patent system's practical operation.

**Patent Thicket**
A dense, overlapping web of patents held by numerous different entities covering components of a single product, making it practically difficult or impossible to verify non-infringement before shipping. Particularly pronounced in software and hardware products (e.g., smartphones) that integrate enormous numbers of individually patented technical components.

**Trade Secret**
Confidential business information (a formula, process, method, technique, or compilation) that provides competitive advantage and is subject to reasonable efforts to maintain secrecy. Unlike copyright and patents, has no fixed term, lasting as long as secrecy is maintained, but provides no protection against independent discovery or lawful reverse engineering.

**Misappropriation (Trade Secret)**
The improper acquisition, disclosure, or use of a trade secret — through theft, breach of a confidentiality obligation, or other improper means. Distinguished from independent discovery or reverse engineering, which do not constitute misappropriation.

**DMCA (Digital Millennium Copyright Act)**
A 1998 US federal law addressing copyright in the digital context, most relevantly through (1) safe harbor provisions (§ 512) protecting online platforms from liability for user-uploaded infringing content, conditioned on a notice-and-takedown process, and (2) anti-circumvention provisions (§ 1201) prohibiting circumvention of technical protection measures controlling access to copyrighted works.

**Notice-and-Takedown**
The process established by DMCA § 512 by which a copyright holder notifies an online platform of allegedly infringing user-uploaded content, triggering an obligation for the platform to promptly remove it to maintain safe harbor protection, subject to a counter-notice process for users who dispute the claim.

**Anti-Circumvention (DMCA § 1201)**
DMCA provisions prohibiting the circumvention of technological protection measures (DRM) controlling access to copyrighted works, and prohibiting the manufacture or distribution of tools primarily designed for such circumvention — with liability analytically independent of whether the underlying use of the copyrighted work would itself be lawful (e.g., fair use).

**Right to Repair**
A policy movement and resulting state-level legislation aimed at ensuring consumers and independent repair providers can access the parts, tools, diagnostic software, and documentation needed to repair devices, specifically countering the use of DMCA anti-circumvention provisions and other technical/legal restrictions to limit repair to manufacturer-authorized channels.
