# Discussion Questions — Week 7 Seminar

---

## Part 1: Copyright and the Idea/Expression Line (~15 min)

1. The idea/expression dichotomy holds that an algorithm is an unprotectable idea, but a specific expression of code implementing it is protected. Can you construct a case where this line is genuinely hard to draw — where "the algorithm" and "the expression of it" are difficult to cleanly separate? What does this suggest about the dichotomy's practical limits?

2. *Google v. Oracle* found API declaring code to be, at most, thinly protected because of its functional nature, and found Google's copying transformative because it enabled a new platform (mobile) rather than substituting for Oracle's existing market. Construct a hypothetical where a company copies a competitor's API for a product that competes *directly* in the same market (not a new platform). Would the fair use analysis come out differently? Walk through the four factors.

3. Copyright terms (life + 70 years, or 95 years for corporate works) were calibrated for literary and artistic works. Should software have a different, shorter copyright term given its much faster practical obsolescence? What would be lost and gained by legislating a software-specific term?

## Part 2: Patents and the Software Patent Debate (~15 min)

4. *Alice*'s two-step test asks whether a claim supplies an "inventive concept" beyond implementing an abstract idea on a generic computer. In your own words, and using an example not from the lecture, describe a software patent claim you believe would pass this test and one you believe would fail it. What's the principled difference between your two examples?

5. Patent trolling and patent thickets are both specific critiques of the software patent system. Are these problems inherent to *software* patents specifically, or are they problems with the patent system generally that happen to manifest severely in software due to the industry's cumulative, componentized nature? Does the distinction matter for what reform would look like?

6. Trade secret protection has no fixed term but provides no protection against independent discovery or reverse engineering; patents have a fixed term but protect against independent invention. If you had developed a genuinely novel and valuable algorithm at a company, which protection regime would you advise pursuing, and what specific facts about the algorithm and its likely reverse-engineerability would change your recommendation?

## Part 3: DMCA and Anti-Circumvention (~15 min)

7. DMCA § 1201 liability for circumventing a technical protection measure is analytically separate from whether the underlying use would be fair use. Is this separation defensible as a policy matter — i.e., is there a coherent reason copyright law would want to punish the *method* of access independently of whether the resulting use is lawful? Or is this simply an overreach that should be corrected?

8. The triennial DMCA exemption rulemaking process grants renewable exemptions (e.g., for security research) rather than permanent statutory ones. What are the advantages of a renewable, re-litigated exemption process compared to a permanent statutory carve-out? What are the costs, particularly for a security researcher trying to plan work years in advance?

9. Right-to-repair laws have emerged specifically to counteract DMCA anti-circumvention being used to restrict independent repair of devices whose underlying purpose (agricultural equipment, smartphones) has nothing to do with copyright's traditional concerns (protecting creative works from piracy). What does this pattern — a legal mechanism being used well outside its original justification — suggest about how technology law evolves, or fails to evolve, relative to new uses of existing statutes?

## Closing

10. The lecture states that the legal status of AI training on copyrighted data is currently unresolved and will likely be substantially clarified during your career. If you were advising an engineering team building a new generative AI product today, what practical steps would you recommend to manage this legal uncertainty, given that the underlying law might change significantly by the time any resulting litigation resolves?
