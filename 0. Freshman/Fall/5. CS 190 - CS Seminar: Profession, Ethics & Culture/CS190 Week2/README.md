### Why This Week Exists

Ethical outcomes are shaped less by individual virtue than by the process a team is embedded in. A deadline structure, a code-review norm, or a licence choice determines what gets caught and what ships. This week examines the dominant development contexts so that later case studies can be read as consequences of process rather than as isolated failures of character.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of Week 2, you should be able to:

1. Distinguish waterfall, Agile, open-source, and research-lab development as distinct working contexts with distinct incentives.
2. Explain why the waterfall model is more often criticised than actually practised.
3. Describe how open-source licensing turns code into a commons, and what obligations that creates.
4. Argue how a given process choice makes specific ethical failures more or less likely.

### How This Course Is Graded (Reminder)

- **Participation (40%):** Weekly seminar attendance and engagement. Quality over quantity — one sharp, well-reasoned comment beats five reflexive ones.
- **Position Papers (60%):** Three 750–1,000 word argumentative papers, assigned Weeks 3, 6 and 9 (20% each). These are argumentative, not summary — you must take and defend a stance. The word limits are hard, and the papers escalate deliberately: #1 supplies the ACM Code as scaffold, #2 removes it, #3 removes the identifiable agent.
