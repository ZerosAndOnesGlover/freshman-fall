**Format:** One page, any format.

## Part A: Raymond vs. Eghbal

In 4–6 sentences, describe the most significant way Eghbal's analysis of open source *updates or contradicts* Raymond's "Cathedral and the Bazaar." Don't summarize both — synthesize them. Where does one's argument require revision in light of the other's evidence?

## Part B: A License Question

You have written a Python library for audio signal processing. It took you eight months. You want to release it as open source. Answer the following, each in 2–3 sentences:

1. Would you choose a copyleft license (GPL/AGPL) or a permissive license (MIT/Apache)? Why?
2. Does your answer change if you learn that three major streaming companies are likely to incorporate your library into commercial products without contributing improvements back?
3. Does your answer change if you learn that researchers studying music cognition at universities are the primary likely users?

There is no single right answer. The quality of your reasoning matters, not the choice you make.

## Part C: Process Observation (if applicable)

If you have any prior experience with a software project — a class project, a personal project, an internship, a hackathon — describe in 3–4 sentences what development process (explicit or implicit) was used and whether it reflected any of the models discussed in this week's lecture. If you have no prior experience, describe what process you *imagine* you would prefer to work in and why.
