# MATH 141 · Lab 12
## Mixed Review and Self-Diagnosis

**Duration:** 2 hours · **Ungraded — attendance only**
**Lab session:** Friday of Week 12

---

## Purpose

This session is diagnostic, not instructional. You will find out, under time pressure and with the
notes closed, which parts of this course you can actually do.

**Bring:** your marked problem sets, both midterms, and a blank sheet for the formula sheet you will
draft at the end.

---

## Part 1: The Cold Start (45 minutes)

Work **Problem Set 12** under exam conditions. Timed, notes closed, no calculator beyond arithmetic.

Do not look at the solutions. Do not look anything up. If you are stuck, write down what you *would*
need to know and move on — that note is the most valuable thing you will produce today.

---

## Part 2: Mark Yourself Honestly (30 minutes)

Mark against the solutions. For every question you did not get fully right, classify the cause:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Did not know the fact** | A formula or theorem statement you could not recall |
| **Knew it, misapplied it** | Right tool, wrong setup — e.g. forgot to split at a crossing |
| **Set-up error** | Wrong integral or wrong region; usually no sketch |
| **Arithmetic** | Method right, computation wrong |

**The classification matters more than the score.** Each category needs a different fix: facts need
the formula sheet, misapplications need worked examples, set-up errors need sketching practice, and
arithmetic needs slowing down.

---

## Part 3: Targeted Revision (30 minutes)

Take the **two** categories where you lost most marks and work only on those.

If you lost most to set-up errors, do five area-and-volume problems and sketch every one. If you lost
most to recall, write out the theorem statements from memory and check them.

Do **not** revise what you already got right. It is comfortable and worthless.

---

## Part 4: Draft the Formula Sheet (15 minutes)

One side of A4, handwritten.

**Include** what you cannot derive: standard derivatives and antiderivatives, the washer and shell
formulas, exact hypotheses of the MVT, IVT and EVT, both parts of the FTC, the Taylor coefficient.

**Exclude** anything you could reconstruct in under a minute. Test three items by trying.

The sheet you produce today is a draft — you will improve it while revising, and the improving is the
point.
