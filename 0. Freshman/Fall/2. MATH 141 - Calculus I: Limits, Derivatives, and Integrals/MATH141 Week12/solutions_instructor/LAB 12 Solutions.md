# MATH 141 · Lab 12 Notes (Instructor)
## Mixed Review and Self-Diagnosis

**This lab is ungraded and diagnostic.** There is nothing to mark. These notes are for running the
session.

---

## Running Part 1 (45 min)

Enforce exam conditions genuinely — notes away, timed, no phones. Students who "just check one
thing" get no diagnostic value from the session, and the whole point is the cold start.

**Circulate but do not help.** When asked, respond: *"Write down what you'd need to know and move
on."* That note is the deliverable.

---

## Running Part 2 (30 min)

The classification matters far more than the score, and students resist it — they would rather total
their marks. Push them to categorise every lost mark.

Expected distribution, from prior cohorts:

| Category | Typical share | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Set-up errors | ~40% | Almost always "did not sketch" |
| Knew it, misapplied it | ~30% | Forgot to split; forgot endpoints; wrong radius |
| Did not know the fact | ~20% | Theorem hypotheses, standard antiderivatives |
| Arithmetic | ~10% | Genuinely least important |

**The intervention that helps most** is pointing out that set-up errors and misapplications together
are ~70% of lost marks, and neither is fixed by more revision of facts. They are fixed by sketching
and by working examples.

| Symptom in PS 12 | Underlying gap | Prescribe |

| D1 answered $0$ | Did not split at the crossing | Five area problems, sketching each |

| C2 not spotted | FTC hypotheses not internalised | Week 9 L2; the sign test |
| C3 gives $16/3$ for distance | Confused displacement with distance | The signed-vs-absolute thread, Weeks 4/9/11 |
| B4 misses endpoints | Only checked critical points | Week 6; the closed-interval method |
| A3 answers 13 | Rounded down | Always round **up** for iteration counts |
| A4 matches slopes only | Forgot continuity is necessary | Week 3 L3 |

## Part 4 — the formula sheet

The exercise is the **audit**, not the sheet. Students routinely include the quotient rule, $\tan'$,
and the washer formula — all derivable in under a minute. Have them test three items by actually
trying to derive them, and remove any they can.

Items genuinely worth keeping: exact hypotheses of MVT/IVT/EVT, both FTC statements, the shell
formula, the Taylor coefficient, and $\tfrac43\pi R^3$ / $\tfrac13\pi r^2h$ as answer checks.

---

## Closing the Session

Worth saying explicitly in the last five minutes:

> The habit this course has been building is *compute it a second way and see whether the answers
> agree*. Two volume methods giving $8\pi$; a derivative rule checked against a central difference; a
> root confirmed by substituting it back. That reflex is more durable than any formula on your sheet,
> and it is what will still be useful in MATH 341 and beyond.
