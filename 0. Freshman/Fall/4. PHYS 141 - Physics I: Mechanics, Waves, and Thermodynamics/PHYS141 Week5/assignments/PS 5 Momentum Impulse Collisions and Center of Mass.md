# PHYS 141 · Problem Set 5
## Momentum, Impulse, Collisions & Center of Mass

**Released:** Friday, Week 5 | **Due:** Friday, Week 6, 11:59 PM
**Instructions:** State clearly which conservation law(s) you are applying. Show all steps. Report answers with correct units, signs, and significant figures.
**Points:** 5 per problem · **100 total**

---

### Part A: Momentum and Impulse (Problems 1–6) — 30 pts

**1.** A 0.14 kg baseball is pitched at 38 m/s and is hit back in the opposite direction at 45 m/s.

(a) Find the change in momentum of the ball.
(b) If the bat is in contact with the ball for 1.5 ms, find the average force exerted on the ball.
(c) Compare this force to the weight of the ball.

---

**2.** A 1000 kg car traveling at 18 m/s comes to a stop in 4.0 s due to braking.

(a) Find the impulse delivered to the car.
(b) Find the average braking force.
(c) If instead the car stopped in 1.0 s (a hard emergency stop), how would your answer to (b) change? Compute the new value.

---

**3.** A 60 kg person jumps from a height of 1.2 m and lands stiff-legged, stopping in 0.05 s.

(a) Find the person's speed just before landing.
(b) Find the impulse delivered to the person during landing.
(c) Find the average force exerted by the ground on the person during landing.
(d) If the person instead bends their knees, extending the stopping time to 0.4 s, find the new average force. Compute the ratio of forces between the two landing styles.

---

**4.** A time-varying force acts on a 5.0 kg object initially at rest: F(t) = 30 − 5t (N, t in seconds), valid for 0 ≤ t ≤ 6 s.

(a) Find the impulse delivered from t=0 to t=6 s (integrate).
(b) Find the object's velocity at t=6 s.
(c) At what time does the force become zero? What is the object's velocity at that time — is it the same as at t=6 s? Explain physically.

---

**5.** A hose sprays water horizontally at a wall at a rate of 3.0 kg/s, with each kg of water hitting at 8.0 m/s and then falling straight down (losing all horizontal velocity). Find the average force exerted on the wall. (Hint: use $\bar F = \Delta p/\Delta t$ with $\Delta p$ being the momentum change of the water striking in a small time interval $\Delta t$, and note the mass flow rate is $\Delta m/\Delta t = 3.0$ kg/s.)

---

**6.** A 0.50 kg ball is dropped from rest and falls 2.0 m onto a hard floor, then bounces straight back up to a height of 1.4 m (some energy is lost in the bounce).

(a) Find the ball's velocity just before impact and just after impact (taking downward as negative).
(b) Find the impulse delivered by the floor to the ball during the bounce.
(c) If the bounce lasts 8.0 ms, find the average force exerted by the floor on the ball. Compare this to the ball's weight.

---

### Part B: Collisions (Problems 7–15) — 45 pts

**7.** A 1500 kg car moving at 12 m/s collides with a stationary 1000 kg car, and they lock together (perfectly inelastic).

(a) Find the common velocity immediately after collision.
(b) Find the kinetic energy before and after the collision.
(c) What fraction of the initial kinetic energy is lost?

---

**8.** Two carts on a frictionless track: Cart A (2.0 kg) moves at 6.0 m/s; Cart B (3.0 kg) moves at −2.0 m/s (toward A). They collide and stick together.

(a) Find the common final velocity (careful with signs).
(b) Find the kinetic energy lost in the collision.

---

**9.** A 0.020 kg bullet is fired into a stationary 2.0 kg wooden block resting on a frictionless surface. The bullet embeds itself, and the block+bullet moves off at 4.0 m/s.

(a) Find the bullet's speed before impact.
(b) Find the kinetic energy lost in the collision. Where did this energy go?

---

**10.** Extend Problem 9: the block (now with embedded bullet) slides across a rough surface (μ_k = 0.30) after the collision. How far does it slide before stopping? (Use the work-energy theorem from Week 4 for this part.)

---

**11.** A 3.0 kg ball moving at 8.0 m/s collides elastically with a stationary 5.0 kg ball.

(a) Use the general elastic collision formulas to find both final velocities.
(b) Verify momentum conservation.
(c) Verify kinetic energy conservation.

---

**12.** A 1.0 kg ball moving at 6.0 m/s collides elastically with a stationary ball of unknown mass m. After the collision, the 1.0 kg ball bounces straight back at 2.0 m/s.

(a) Use momentum conservation to find the final velocity of the unknown mass in terms of m.
(b) Use the elastic collision energy condition (or the general formula) to solve for m.
(c) Verify your answer using the general elastic collision formula for v_{A,f} directly.

---

**13.** A 2.0 kg object moving at 5.0 m/s in the +x direction strikes a stationary 4.0 kg object. After the collision, the 2.0 kg object moves at 2.0 m/s at 60° above the +x axis.

(a) Find the velocity (magnitude and direction) of the 4.0 kg object after collision.
(b) Determine whether this collision is elastic, inelastic, or perfectly inelastic by comparing KE before and after.

---

**14. (Ballistic pendulum — classic synthesis)** A 0.010 kg bullet is fired into a 2.0 kg wooden block hanging from a string (a ballistic pendulum), initially at rest. The block+bullet swings up to a maximum height of 0.12 m.

(a) Use energy conservation (Week 4 methods) to find the speed of the block+bullet system immediately after the bullet embeds.
(b) Use momentum conservation (perfectly inelastic collision) to find the speed of the bullet before impact.
(c) Find the kinetic energy lost in the collision (this energy is "used up" deforming the wood and bullet, generating heat — it does NOT appear in the pendulum's swing height, which is governed by the post-collision KE only).

---

**15.** A firework of mass 2.0 kg is launched straight up and, at the peak of its trajectory (v=0 at that instant), explodes into two fragments. Fragment 1 (0.8 kg) flies off horizontally at 15 m/s. Find the velocity (magnitude and direction) of fragment 2 (1.2 kg).

### Part C: Center of Mass (Problems 16–20) — 25 pts

**16.** Three point masses lie on the x-axis: 2.0 kg at x=1.0 m, 3.0 kg at x=4.0 m, 5.0 kg at x=8.0 m. Find the center of mass.

---

**17.** A 4.0 kg object is at (2.0, 3.0) m and a 6.0 kg object is at (8.0, −1.0) m.

(a) Find the center of mass position.
(b) If the 4.0 kg object moves at (3.0, 0) m/s and the 6.0 kg object moves at (−1.0, 2.0) m/s, find the velocity of the center of mass.
(c) Find the total momentum of the system using $\vec p_{total} = M\vec v_{cm}$, and verify it matches the sum of individual momenta.

---

**18.** A uniform rod of length L = 3.0 m and mass 6.0 kg lies along the x-axis from x=0 to x=3.0 m. A point mass of 4.0 kg is attached at x = 3.0 m (the end of the rod).

(a) Find the center of mass of the rod alone (use symmetry).
(b) Find the center of mass of the rod+point-mass system.

---

**19.** A 1200 kg cannon (initially at rest, free to recoil on a frictionless surface) fires a 8.0 kg cannonball horizontally at 120 m/s.

(a) Use momentum conservation to find the recoil velocity of the cannon.
(b) Find the center of mass velocity of the cannon+cannonball system before and after firing. Explain why this makes sense given there are no external horizontal forces.
(c) Find the kinetic energy of the cannon and of the cannonball after firing. Where did this energy come from? (Hint: think about the chemical energy of the gunpowder — this is analogous to an "explosion," the reverse of a perfectly inelastic collision.)

---

**20. (Synthesis)** A 70 kg person stands at one end of a 4.0 m long, 120 kg boat, initially at rest in still water (ignore water resistance/friction). The person walks to the other end of the boat.

(a) Using the fact that the center of mass of the person+boat system cannot move (no external horizontal forces, frictionless water), find how far the boat moves relative to the water.
(b) Find how far the person moves relative to the water.
(c) Verify that the person's displacement relative to the boat is indeed 4.0 m (the length of the boat), consistent with your answers to (a) and (b).
(d) If the person then walks back to the original end, where does the boat end up relative to its starting position?
