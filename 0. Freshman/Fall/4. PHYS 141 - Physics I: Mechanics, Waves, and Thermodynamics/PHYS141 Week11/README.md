## Learning Objectives

By the end of Week 11, you will be able to:

1. State the Zeroth Law and explain why it is what makes thermometry meaningful
2. Convert between Celsius, Fahrenheit, and kelvin, and say when each is admissible in a formula
3. Apply $\Delta L = \alpha L_0\Delta T$ and derive $\beta = 3\alpha$
4. Explain why a hole in a heated plate expands, using the filled-disc argument
5. Explain water's anomalous expansion and its consequences for lakes and life
6. Distinguish heat from temperature and from internal energy, in both words and units
7. Apply $Q = mc\Delta T$ and $Q = mL$, and construct a complete heating curve
8. Solve calorimetry problems, **including checking whether all ice melts**
9. Compare conduction, convection, and radiation, and identify which dominates in a given situation
10. Apply $P = kA\Delta T/L$ to single- and multi-layer walls
11. Apply $P = e\sigma A T^4$ and compute net radiative exchange
12. Apply $PV = nRT$ and its special cases, always in kelvin
13. State the kinetic-theory result $\tfrac12m\overline{v^2} = \tfrac32k_BT$ and explain what temperature fundamentally measures

## Key Results

| | |
|---|---|
| Zeroth Law | Thermal equilibrium is transitive ⟹ temperature exists |
| Kelvin | $T_K = T_C + 273.15$; **required** in gas laws and radiation |
| C and F coincide | at $-40°$ |
| Linear expansion | $\Delta L = \alpha L_0\Delta T$; $\beta = 3\alpha$ |
| 1 km steel bridge over 40 K | expands **0.48 m** |
| Hole in a heated plate | gets **bigger** |
| Water densest at | **4°C** — lakes freeze top-down |
| Heat | energy **in transit**, in joules; objects contain *internal energy* |
| $c_{\text{water}}$ | 4186 J·kg⁻¹K⁻¹ — exceptionally high |
| Latent heat | $Q = mL$; temperature constant |
| $L_v/L_f$ for water | **6.77** — why steam burns badly |
| Calorimetry | $\sum Q = 0$; **check ice fully melts** |
| Conduction | $P = kA\Delta T/L$; $k$ spans $10^4$ |
| Insulators | work by trapping **still air** |
| Radiation | $P = e\sigma AT^4$; doubling $T$ gives $16\times$ |
| Person at 20°C | radiates a **net 185 W** |
| Ideal gas | $PV = nRT$; 22.41 L per mole at STP |
| Kinetic theory | $\tfrac12m\overline{v^2} = \tfrac32k_BT$ — **temperature is average KE** |

## Connections

**Back:** Week 4's energy conservation is what calorimetry applies. Week 5's impulse is what kinetic
theory sums over molecular collisions to obtain pressure. Week 10's temperature-dependent sound speed
is explained here — it is the molecular speed that sets it.

**Forward:** Week 12 adds the First and Second Laws, entropy, and heat engines. The ideal gas
introduced today is the working substance throughout.

**Sideways:** MATH 151's equivalence relations are exactly what the Zeroth Law asserts about thermal
equilibrium. The $T^4$ radiation law governs everything from CPU cooling to stellar structure, and the
$k$ values in this week's tables are what every thermal-design decision in engineering rests on.
