## Temperature, Heat, and Thermal Expansion

**Released:** Friday EOD, Week 11 | **Due:** Friday, Week 12, start of class
**Total: 100 points**

**Instructions**
- Show all working. A bare numerical answer earns at most half marks.
- **Use kelvin in every gas-law and radiation calculation.** Celsius is acceptable only for
  temperature *differences*.
- Constants: $c_{\text{water}} = 4186$, $c_{\text{ice}} = 2090$, $c_{\text{Al}} = 900$,

## Part A — Temperature and Expansion (25 points)

**1.** *(4 pts)* Convert $-15.0°$C to Fahrenheit and kelvin, and $98.6°$F to Celsius. At what
temperature do the Celsius and Fahrenheit scales read the same?

**2.** *(4 pts)* A steel railway rail is 25.000 m long at $10.0°$C. Find its length at $45.0°$C.

**3.** *(4 pts)* An aluminium rod measures 2.0000 m at $20.0°$C. At what temperature is it
2.0050 m long?

**4.** *(5 pts)* A steel bridge is 1.20 km long. Find its total expansion between $-10.0°$C and
$40.0°$C, and comment on the engineering consequence.

**5.** *(4 pts)* A glass container holds exactly 500.0 mL at $20.0°$C. Find its capacity at $80.0°$C.

**6.** *(4 pts)* A steel ring of inner diameter 4.000 cm must be fitted over a shaft of diameter
4.010 cm. To what temperature must the ring be heated, starting from $20.0°$C?

---

## Part B — Heat and Calorimetry (30 points)

**7.** *(4 pts)* How much energy raises 2.50 kg of water from $15.0°$C to $85.0°$C?

**8.** *(4 pts)* A 0.400 kg copper block absorbs 12.0 kJ. Find its temperature rise.

**9.** *(6 pts)* Find the total energy needed to convert 0.250 kg of ice at $-10.0°$C into steam at
$100°$C. Present your answer as a table of the four stages, and state what fraction is used in
boiling.

**10.** *(5 pts)* A 0.150 kg aluminium block at $150°$C is dropped into 0.400 kg of water at
$18.0°$C. Find the final temperature, and verify that heat lost equals heat gained.

**11.** *(6 pts)* 0.080 kg of ice at $0°$C is added to 0.250 kg of water at $30.0°$C. **First
determine whether all the ice melts**, showing the comparison, then find the final temperature.

**12.** *(5 pts)* A 0.500 kg lead block at $200°$C is dropped into 0.200 kg of water at $20.0°$C.
Find the final temperature, and explain why it lies so close to the water's starting value.

## Part C — Heat Transfer (25 points)

**13.** *(5 pts)* A single-glazed window of area 2.00 m² and thickness 4.00 mm has $k = 0.800$.
Find the conduction rate with 20.0°C inside and 0°C outside.

**14.** *(7 pts)* Repeat for double glazing: two 4.00 mm panes separated by a 10.0 mm still-air gap
($k_{\text{air}} = 0.026$). Treat the three layers as thermal resistances in series. By what factor
is the heat loss reduced, and which layer dominates?

**15.** *(5 pts)* A blackbody sphere of radius 0.100 m is at 500 K. Find its radiated power.

**16.** *(5 pts)* A person of surface area 1.70 m² and emissivity 0.97 at skin temperature $33.0°$C
stands in a room at $18.0°$C. Find the **net** radiated power, and comment on its size relative to a
typical metabolic output of about 100 W.

**17.** *(3 pts)* Explain how a vacuum flask suppresses all three heat-transfer mechanisms.

---

## Part D — The Ideal Gas (20 points)

**18.** *(5 pts)* A gas occupies 0.0500 m³ at $1.50\times10^5$ Pa and 300 K. Find the number of
moles, and the pressure after isothermal compression to 0.0200 m³.

**19.** *(4 pts)* A sealed rigid container of gas is heated from $20.0°$C to $120°$C. By what factor
does the pressure rise? Show explicitly why using Celsius gives the wrong answer.

**20.** *(5 pts)* Find the rms speed of a nitrogen molecule ($M = 0.0280$ kg/mol) and of a hydrogen
molecule ($M = 0.00202$ kg/mol), both at 300 K. Comment on the ratio.

**21.** *(3 pts)* Find the volume of one mole of ideal gas at $0°$C and 1.00 atm ($1.013\times10^5$ Pa).

**22.** *(3 pts)* Two gases are at the same temperature. Compare their average molecular kinetic
energies and their average speeds, and explain why the answers differ.
