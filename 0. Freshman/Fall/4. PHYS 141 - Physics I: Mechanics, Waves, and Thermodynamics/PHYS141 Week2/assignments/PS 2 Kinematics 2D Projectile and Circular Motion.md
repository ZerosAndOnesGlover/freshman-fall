### Part A: 2D Kinematics — Vectors and Components (Problems 1–5) — 25 pts

**1.** A particle's position vector is r⃗(t) = (2t² − 3t)x̂ + (t³ − 4t)ŷ meters.

(a) Find v⃗(t) and a⃗(t).
(b) At t = 2 s, find the speed and the direction of velocity (angle from +x axis).
(c) At t = 2 s, find |a⃗| and its direction.
(d) Is this constant-acceleration motion? Justify.

---

**2.** A hockey puck slides across frictionless ice with initial velocity v⃗₀ = (5.0, 3.0) m/s. A constant force gives it acceleration a⃗ = (−0.5, 0.8) m/s².

(a) Write equations for x(t), y(t), v_x(t), v_y(t).
(b) Find the position and speed at t = 4.0 s.
(c) At what time does the puck have zero x-velocity? What is its total speed at that moment?
(d) Sketch the trajectory qualitatively — indicate the initial direction, how it curves, and where zero x-velocity occurs.

---

**3.** A particle starts at (3, −2) m with velocity (−4, 6) m/s and constant acceleration (2, −3) m/s².

(a) Find the position at t = 3 s.
(b) Find the time(s) when the particle crosses the x-axis (y = 0).
(c) At the first x-axis crossing, what is the velocity vector?

---

**4.** A river is 120 m wide. A boat can travel at 4.0 m/s relative to the water. The river current flows at 2.5 m/s downstream.

(a) If the boat aims directly perpendicular to the bank, find the actual velocity relative to the ground, its magnitude, and direction.
(b) How far downstream does the boat land?
(c) How long does the crossing take?
(d) At what upstream angle must the boat aim in order to travel straight across (perpendicular to bank, zero downstream drift)? Is this possible given the speeds? Show your reasoning.

---

**5.** Two particles A and B move as follows:
- A: starts at origin, v⃗_A = (6, 0) m/s, a⃗_A = (0, 2) m/s²
- B: starts at (24, 0) m, v⃗_B = (−3, 0) m/s, a⃗_B = (0, 0)

(a) Write position vectors r⃗_A(t) and r⃗_B(t).
(b) Find the time when the x-coordinates are equal.
(c) At that time, are the particles at the same location? Find the separation vector r⃗_A − r⃗_B.
(d) Do the particles ever occupy exactly the same point? If so, when?

### Part B: Projectile Motion (Problems 6–14) — 45 pts

**6.** A ball is launched at 20 m/s at 60° above horizontal from ground level.

(a) Find the initial x and y components of velocity.
(b) Find the time of flight.
(c) Find the maximum height.
(d) Find the horizontal range.
(e) Find the velocity vector (magnitude and direction) at t = 1.5 s.

---

**7.** A soccer ball is kicked from ground level and has a range of 40 m and a maximum height of 10 m. Find:

(a) The time of flight. *(Hint: use H = v₀ᵧ²/2g and R = v₀ₓ · T together.)*
(b) The initial speed v₀.
(c) The launch angle θ.

---

**8.** A stone is thrown horizontally from the top of a 45 m cliff at 15 m/s.

(a) How long until it hits the ground?
(b) How far from the base of the cliff does it land?
(c) What is the velocity vector at impact (magnitude and direction below horizontal)?
(d) At what point along the trajectory is the speed equal to twice the launch speed?

---

**9.** A projectile is launched at 35 m/s. You want it to hit a target 80 m away at the same height.

(a) Find both launch angles that achieve this.
(b) Find the time of flight for each angle.
(c) For each trajectory, find the maximum height reached.
(d) Which trajectory would be preferred in a real-world situation where the target is behind a 5 m wall located 40 m away? Justify geometrically.

---

**10.** A long-jumper leaves the ground at 9.0 m/s at 25° above horizontal.

(a) What is the range of the jump?
(b) What would the range be at the optimal 45° angle with the same launch speed?
(c) Real athletes jump at angles between 15° and 25°, not 45°. Using your answers to (a) and (b), state the range penalty. Then propose a physical reason why 45° is not used in practice. *(Hint: think about what it takes to achieve a high launch angle.)*

---

**11.** A cannon fires a ball at 120 m/s and must hit a target on a hill 800 m away horizontally and 150 m above the cannon.

(a) Setting up the trajectory equation y = xtanθ − gx²/(2v₀²cos²θ), substitute the target coordinates to get an equation in tanθ. Let u = tanθ to simplify.
(b) Solve the resulting quadratic for the two possible values of u, and hence the two launch angles.
(c) Which angle is more practical? Explain.

---

**12.** A ball is thrown from the edge of a building of height 20 m at 15 m/s at 30° **below** horizontal. Taking the ground as y = 0 and the launch point as the origin (so the ground is at y = −20 m):

(a) Write x(t) and y(t) with correct signs for this downward-angled launch.
(b) Find when the ball hits the ground.
(c) Find the range (horizontal distance traveled).
(d) Find the speed on impact.

---

**13. (Synthesis — Monkey and Hunter)** A classic problem: a hunter aims directly at a monkey hanging in a tree 30 m away (horizontal distance) and 15 m above the ground. At the instant the gun fires, the monkey drops from the tree in free fall (it sees the flash). Does the bullet hit the monkey, assuming the bullet's speed is fast enough to reach the tree?

(a) Show algebraically (without numbers) that the bullet and monkey are always at the same height at the same horizontal position, regardless of v₀, as long as v₀ is large enough. *(Let the gun be at the origin, aimed at the monkey's initial position (d, h). Write y_bullet(t) and y_monkey(t) and compare them at the time the bullet reaches x = d.)*
(b) What is the minimum v₀ for the bullet to reach the tree before hitting the ground?
(c) What happens if v₀ is too small — does the bullet pass above or below the monkey? Explain.

---

**14. (Estimation)** A baseball pitcher throws at 95 mph (42.5 m/s). The pitcher's mound is 18.4 m from home plate. The ball is released at a height of 1.8 m and the catcher's mitt is at 0.9 m height. Treat the pitch as projectile motion (ignore spin and air effects).

(a) Estimate the launch angle needed (below horizontal) to arrive at the correct height.
(b) How much does the ball drop during the pitch?
(c) At the release angle you found, how long does the pitch take?
(d) Comment on whether ignoring air resistance is a good approximation for a 95 mph fastball over 18.4 m.

### Part C: Circular Motion (Problems 15–20) — 30 pts

**15.** A wheel of radius 0.40 m spins at 300 rpm.

(a) Convert 300 rpm to rad/s.
(b) Find the tangential speed of a point on the rim.
(c) Find the centripetal acceleration of a point on the rim.
(d) Express the centripetal acceleration as a multiple of g.

---

**16.** The Moon orbits Earth with a period of 27.3 days at a mean distance of 3.84 × 10⁸ m.

(a) Find the Moon's orbital speed.
(b) Find its centripetal acceleration.
(c) Compare this to g = 9.81 m/s² — the ratio should be approximately (R_Earth/R_Moon_orbit)². Check this and explain why Newton found this significant. *(R_Earth = 6.37 × 10⁶ m)*

---

**17.** A car goes around a circular curve of radius 150 m. The coefficient of static friction between tires and road is 0.70 (this will be needed in Week 3, but for now just use it to note that the maximum centripetal acceleration before skidding is μₛg = 0.70 × 9.81).

(a) What is the maximum speed at which the car can safely navigate the curve?
(b) At that maximum speed, what is the centripetal acceleration?
(c) If the car travels at 80 km/h, is it safe? Show the calculation.

---

**18.** A point on a rotating disk starts from rest and reaches ω = 25 rad/s in 10 s with constant angular acceleration.

(a) Find the angular acceleration α.
(b) How many revolutions does it make during those 10 s?
(c) At t = 10 s, a point 0.20 m from the center has what tangential speed, centripetal acceleration, and tangential acceleration?
(d) What is the magnitude and direction of the total acceleration of that point at t = 10 s?

---

**19.** A satellite orbits Earth at altitude h = 400 km above the surface. R_Earth = 6.371 × 10⁶ m, g_surface = 9.81 m/s².

At altitude h, gravity gives centripetal acceleration a_c = g_surface × (R_E/(R_E + h))².

(a) Compute a_c at 400 km altitude.
(b) Using a_c = v²/r, find the orbital speed.
(c) Find the orbital period. Compare to 90 minutes (the well-known approximate ISS period).
(d) Find the centripetal acceleration due to Earth's rotation for someone on the equatorial surface. What fraction of g is this? (This explains why the effective g at the equator is slightly less than at the poles.)

---

**20. (Synthesis)** A roller-coaster loop has radius r = 12 m. The car moves at constant speed (ignore friction and gravity variations around the loop).

(a) At the bottom of the loop, draw a free-body diagram and identify which direction centripetal acceleration points.
(b) At the top of the loop, draw a free-body diagram and identify the centripetal direction.
(c) The minimum speed at the top for the car to maintain contact with the track occurs when the normal force N = 0. Using N + mg = mv²/r (at top), find v_min.
(d) If the car travels at 1.5 × v_min, find the total acceleration magnitude at the top (it equals a_c since the loop is traversed at constant speed — but express a_c in terms of g for physical intuition).
(e) Why is the constraint at the top more stringent than at the bottom? (Think about which direction gravity helps vs. hinders providing centripetal force.)
