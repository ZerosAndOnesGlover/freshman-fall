### Part A: Angular Momentum (Problems 1–3) — 30 pts

**1.** A 0.30 kg particle moves in a circle of radius 1.5 m at 6.0 m/s. Find its angular momentum about the center of the circle.

---

**2.** A solid disk (M=5.0 kg, R=0.40 m) spins at ω=10 rad/s. Find its angular momentum.

---

**3.** A 2.0 kg particle at position (3.0, 0, 0) m has velocity (0, 5.0, 0) m/s.

(a) Find its angular momentum vector about the origin.
(b) A force F⃗=(0,0,10) N (in the z-direction) acts on it. Find the torque about the origin due to this force, and verify it is consistent with how L would need to change (conceptually — you don't need to integrate, just check the direction makes sense given τ=dL/dt).

---

### Part B: Conservation of Angular Momentum (Problems 4–7) — 40 pts

**4.** A skater spins at 3.0 rad/s with arms extended, $I_i=5.0$ kg·m². Pulling arms in reduces I to 2.0 kg·m².

(a) Find the new angular velocity.
(b) Find the kinetic energy before and after. Where does the energy change come from?

---

**5.** A 60 kg person stands at the center of a rotating platform (I_platform=200 kg·m², person's I≈0 at center) spinning at 2.0 rad/s. The person walks out to r=1.5 m from the center.

(a) Find the new angular velocity (treat the person as a point mass once at radius r).
(b) Find the fractional decrease in angular velocity.

---

**6.** A disk (I₁=0.60 kg·m²) spins at ω₀=12 rad/s. A second, identical but stationary disk (I₂=0.60 kg·m²) is dropped onto it and they stick together (same axis).

(a) Find the common final angular velocity.
(b) Find the kinetic energy lost in this rotational "collision."
(c) Compare this problem's structure to a linear perfectly-inelastic collision between two equal masses, one initially at rest. What is the analogous fractional KE loss in the linear case (from Week 5), and does it match your answer here?

---

**7.** A star of radius $R_0=7\times10^8$ m (roughly the Sun's radius) rotates once every 25 days. It collapses (supernova) to a neutron star of radius $R_f=12$ km, conserving both mass and angular momentum, with moment of inertia $I=\frac{2}{5}MR^2$ (uniform sphere) before and after.

(a) Find the ratio $I_f/I_i$ in terms of $(R_f/R_0)^2$.
(b) Find the new rotation period, using $I_i\omega_i=I_f\omega_f$ and $\omega=2\pi/T$.
(c) Comment on whether this rotation rate is physically plausible for a real neutron star (compare to observed millisecond pulsars, which rotate hundreds of times per second).

---

### Part C: Static Equilibrium (Problems 8–10) — 30 pts

**8.** A uniform beam of length 8.0 m and weight 600 N is supported at both ends. A 900 N weight is placed 3.0 m from the left end. Find the force exerted by each support.

---

**9.** A 4.0 m uniform ladder weighing 180 N leans against a frictionless wall at 65° from the horizontal. Find the normal force from the wall, the normal force from the floor, and the minimum coefficient of static friction at the floor to prevent slipping.

---

**10.** A horizontal uniform beam of length 5.0 m and weight 300 N is hinged to a wall at one end. A cable from the far end makes a 35° angle with the beam and is anchored to the wall above the hinge. No additional load hangs from the beam.

(a) Find the tension in the cable.
(b) Find the horizontal and vertical components of the hinge force.
(c) Find the magnitude of the total hinge force.
