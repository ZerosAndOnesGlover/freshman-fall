## Part A — Wave Properties (25 points)

**1.** *(5 pts)* A wave has $f = 512$ Hz and $\lambda = 0.664$ m. Find $v$, $T$, $k$, and $\omega$.

**2.** *(5 pts)* A string has $\mu = 8.00$ g/m under 120 N of tension. Find the wave speed. What
tension would be needed to double it? Comment on whether that is practical.

**3.** *(5 pts)* A wave is described by $y = (0.0200~\text{m})\sin(4.00x - 60.0t)$, SI units. State
$A$, $\lambda$, $k$, $f$, $T$, $v$, and the direction of travel.

**4.** *(5 pts)* A 2.50 m string of mass 12.0 g is under 150 N tension. Find $v$ and the time for a
pulse to traverse the string.

**5.** *(5 pts)* A string wave has $A = 3.00$ mm, $f = 120$ Hz, $\mu = 6.00$ g/m, and $v = 90.0$ m/s.
Find the transmitted power. What amplitude would triple it?

---

## Part B — Superposition and Interference (25 points)

**6.** *(5 pts)* Two waves of amplitude 4.00 cm interfere with a phase difference of $\pi/3$. Find
the resultant amplitude. At what phase difference would they cancel completely?

**7.** *(6 pts)* Two sources emit 500 Hz sound in phase. Find the three smallest path differences
producing (a) constructive and (b) destructive interference.

**8.** *(6 pts)* Two speakers 3.00 m apart emit 1.20 kHz in phase. A listener stands 4.00 m from one
speaker and 4.85 m from the other. Compute the path difference in wavelengths and state whether the
position is loud or quiet.

**9.** *(4 pts)* Two waves interfere destructively so the displacement is zero throughout the overlap
region. Explain what has become of the energy, and why this does not violate conservation of energy.

**10.** *(4 pts)* A pulse travels along a rope towards a **fixed** end. Sketch it before and after
reflection and state precisely what changes. Repeat for a **free** end.

---

## Part C — Beats (15 points)

**11.** *(5 pts)* Two tuning forks produce 5 beats per second; one is 512 Hz. Give both possible
frequencies for the other, and describe an experiment that would decide between them.

**12.** *(5 pts)* A guitarist hears 4 beats per second against a 330 Hz reference, and the beats slow
when she loosens the string. Was her string sharp or flat? What was its frequency?

**13.** *(5 pts)* Derive the beat frequency $f_{\text{beat}} = \lvert f_1 - f_2\rvert$ from the
superposition of two cosines, explaining carefully why the audible beat rate is the **difference**
and not half of it.

## Part D — Standing Waves (35 points)

**14.** *(5 pts)* A 0.650 m string fixed at both ends carries waves at 240 m/s. Find $f_1$ and the
first four harmonics.

**15.** *(5 pts)* A string's third harmonic is 420 Hz. Find its fundamental and its fifth harmonic.

**16.** *(6 pts)* A 1.50 m string has $\mu = 4.00$ g/m under 100 N tension. Find $v$, $\lambda_1$, and
$f_1$.

**17.** *(5 pts)* A string 0.800 m long vibrates in its fourth harmonic. Give the positions of every
node and antinode.

**18.** *(6 pts)* A guitar string sounds 196 Hz open. Where must a fret be placed to sound 262 Hz?
Express the answer as a fraction of the string length measured from the nut.

**19.** *(4 pts)* By what factor must the tension of a 440 Hz string change to raise it to 494 Hz?
Comment on whether tuning by tension alone is practical over a wide range.

**20.** *(4 pts)* Explain why a string fixed at both ends vibrates only at discrete frequencies,
while a wave on an unbounded string may have any frequency. State what physical requirement does the
quantising.
