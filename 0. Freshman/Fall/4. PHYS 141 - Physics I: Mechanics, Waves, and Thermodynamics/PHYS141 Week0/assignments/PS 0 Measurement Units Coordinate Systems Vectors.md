### Part A: Units & Dimensional Analysis (Problems 1–6) — 30 pts

**1.** Derive the SI base units of the following derived quantities, showing your work:
(a) Angular momentum (kg·m²/s, but derive it from L = r × p)
(b) Electric field (from F = qE)
(c) The gravitational constant G (from F = Gm₁m₂/r²)

**2.** The kinetic energy of an object is given by KE = ½mv². Verify this is dimensionally consistent with the SI unit of energy (the Joule).

**3.** A student proposes the formula for the period of a mass-spring oscillator: T = 2π√(m/k), where m is mass and k is the spring constant (units N/m). Use dimensional analysis to verify or refute this formula.

**4.** The drag force on an object moving through a fluid is sometimes modeled as F = ½ρv²C_dA, where ρ is fluid density (kg/m³), v is speed, C_d is a dimensionless drag coefficient, and A is cross-sectional area. Verify this equation is dimensionally consistent with force.

**5.** Convert the following (show your unit-cancellation work explicitly):
(a) 88 km/h to m/s
(b) 1 light-year (the distance light travels in 1 year) to meters, then to miles
(c) A pressure of 1 atm = 101,325 Pa to pounds per square inch (1 lb = 4.448 N, 1 inch = 0.0254 m)

**6.** Using only dimensional analysis, determine how the orbital period T of a planet depends on its orbital radius r and the mass of the central star M, given that gravitational acceleration involves G (with dimensions L³M⁻¹T⁻²). Assume T = C·rᵃ·Mᵇ·Gᶜ for some dimensionless constant C and find a, b, c.

### Part B: Significant Figures & Estimation (Problems 7–10) — 20 pts

**7.** Perform the following calculations and report your answer with the correct number of significant figures:
(a) 4.52 × 3.1
(b) 18.0 / 3.652
(c) 12.65 + 1.2 + 0.075
(d) (2.50 × 10³) × (1.2 × 10⁻⁵)

**8.** A rectangular plate measures 12.34 cm by 5.2 cm. Calculate its area with the correct number of significant figures, and explain in one sentence why your answer has that many sig figs.

**9. (Fermi Problem)** Estimate the total mass of all the water in Earth's oceans, using only order-of-magnitude reasoning (no looking up exact values). Show your reasoning step by step: what intermediate quantities did you estimate, and what assumptions did you make? Compare your final order of magnitude to the accepted value (~1.4 × 10²¹ kg) and discuss any discrepancy.

**10. (Fermi Problem)** Estimate how many times your heart will beat in your lifetime. Show all reasoning steps.

### Part C: Coordinate Systems (Problems 11–15) — 25 pts

**11.** Convert the Cartesian point (-3, 4) to polar coordinates (r, θ). Be careful with the quadrant.

**12.** Convert the polar point (r, θ) = (8, 210°) to Cartesian coordinates.

**13.** A point in 3D space has spherical coordinates (r, θ, φ) = (6, 45°, 120°) using the physics convention (θ from z-axis, φ in xy-plane). Find its Cartesian coordinates.

**14.** Convert the Cartesian point (1, 1, 1) to spherical coordinates. Show your work for r, θ, and φ.

**15.** Explain, in your own words (3–5 sentences), why the unit vector θ̂ in polar coordinates is NOT a constant vector, while x̂ in Cartesian coordinates is. What practical consequence does this have when computing derivatives of position in polar coordinates (a topic we will cover formally in Week 2)?

### Part D: Vectors (Problems 16–20) — 25 pts

**16.** Given A⃗ = (2, -1, 3) and B⃗ = (-4, 0, 2):
(a) Find A⃗ + B⃗ and A⃗ - B⃗
(b) Find |A⃗| and |B⃗|
(c) Find the unit vector in the direction of A⃗

**17.** Find the angle between the vectors A⃗ = (1, 2, 2) and B⃗ = (2, 0, -1) using the dot product.

**18.** A force F⃗ = (5, 3, 0) N acts on an object that moves through a displacement d⃗ = (2, -1, 0) m. Calculate the work done, W = F⃗·d⃗. Is the work positive, negative, or zero — and what does the sign mean physically?

**19.** Compute the cross product A⃗ × B⃗ where A⃗ = (1, 0, 0) and B⃗ = (0, 1, 0). Verify your answer satisfies the right-hand rule. Then compute B⃗ × A⃗ and confirm the anti-commutative property.

**20. (Synthesis problem)** A wrench applies a force of F⃗ = (0, -20, 0) N at the position r⃗ = (0.3, 0, 0) m relative to a bolt (the pivot point).
(a) Calculate the torque vector τ⃗ = r⃗ × F⃗.
(b) What is the magnitude of the torque?
(c) If the same force were applied at r⃗ = (0, 0.3, 0) instead (i.e., directly "above" the pivot, parallel to the force direction), what would the torque be? Explain physically why this makes sense in terms of the geometric meaning of the cross product.
