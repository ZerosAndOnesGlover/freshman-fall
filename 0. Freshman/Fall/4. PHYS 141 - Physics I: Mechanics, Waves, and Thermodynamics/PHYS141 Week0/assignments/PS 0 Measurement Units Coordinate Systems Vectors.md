### Part A: Units & Dimensional Analysis (Problems 1–4) — 40 pts

**1.** Derive the SI base units of the following derived quantities, showing your work:
(a) Angular momentum (kg·m²/s, but derive it from L = r × p)
(b) Electric field (from F = qE)
(c) The gravitational constant G (from F = Gm₁m₂/r²)

**2.** A student proposes the formula for the period of a mass-spring oscillator: T = 2π√(m/k), where m is mass and k is the spring constant (units N/m). Use dimensional analysis to verify or refute this formula.

**3.** Convert the following (show your unit-cancellation work explicitly):
(a) 88 km/h to m/s
(b) 1 light-year (the distance light travels in 1 year) to meters, then to miles
(c) A pressure of 1 atm = 101,325 Pa to pounds per square inch (1 lb = 4.448 N, 1 inch = 0.0254 m)

**4.** Using only dimensional analysis, determine how the orbital period T of a planet depends on its orbital radius r and the mass of the central star M, given that gravitational acceleration involves G (with dimensions L³M⁻¹T⁻²). Assume T = C·rᵃ·Mᵇ·Gᶜ for some dimensionless constant C and find a, b, c.

### Part B: Significant Figures & Estimation (Problems 5–6) — 20 pts

**5.** Perform the following calculations and report your answer with the correct number of significant figures:
(a) 4.52 × 3.1
(b) 18.0 / 3.652
(c) 12.65 + 1.2 + 0.075
(d) (2.50 × 10³) × (1.2 × 10⁻⁵)

**6. (Fermi Problem)** Estimate the total mass of all the water in Earth's oceans, using only order-of-magnitude reasoning (no looking up exact values). Show your reasoning step by step: what intermediate quantities did you estimate, and what assumptions did you make? Compare your final order of magnitude to the accepted value (~1.4 × 10²¹ kg) and discuss any discrepancy.

### Part C: Coordinate Systems (Problems 7–8) — 20 pts

**7.** Convert the Cartesian point (-3, 4) to polar coordinates (r, θ). Be careful with the quadrant.

**8.** Explain, in your own words (3–5 sentences), why the unit vector θ̂ in polar coordinates is NOT a constant vector, while x̂ in Cartesian coordinates is. What practical consequence does this have when computing derivatives of position in polar coordinates (a topic we will cover formally in Week 2)?

### Part D: Vectors (Problems 9–10) — 20 pts

**9.** Find the angle between the vectors A⃗ = (1, 2, 2) and B⃗ = (2, 0, -1) using the dot product.

**10. (Synthesis problem)** A wrench applies a force of F⃗ = (0, -20, 0) N at the position r⃗ = (0.3, 0, 0) m relative to a bolt (the pivot point).
(a) Calculate the torque vector τ⃗ = r⃗ × F⃗.
(b) What is the magnitude of the torque?
(c) If the same force were applied at r⃗ = (0, 0.3, 0) instead (i.e., directly "above" the pivot, parallel to the force direction), what would the torque be? Explain physically why this makes sense in terms of the geometric meaning of the cross product.
