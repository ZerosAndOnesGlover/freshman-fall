### Part A: Rotational Kinematics (Problems 1–6) — 30 pts

**1.** A wheel rotates from rest with constant angular acceleration 4.0 rad/s².

(a) Find ω at t = 5.0 s.
(b) Find the angular displacement at t = 5.0 s.
(c) Find the number of revolutions completed.

---

**2.** A fan blade slows from 1800 rpm to 600 rpm in 8.0 s (constant angular deceleration).

(a) Convert both speeds to rad/s.
(b) Find the angular acceleration.
(c) Find the angular displacement during this interval, in radians and in revolutions.

---

**3.** A point on a rotating wheel of radius 0.40 m has a tangential speed of 6.0 m/s and a centripetal acceleration of 45 m/s².

(a) Find the angular velocity ω.
(b) Find the period of rotation T.
(c) Verify using ω = v/r.

---

**4.** A CD spins such that a point 0.030 m from the center has constant linear speed 1.2 m/s (CDs are actually read at constant *linear* velocity, requiring the angular velocity to change as the read head moves outward — but for this problem, treat the disk as spinning at whatever ω gives this linear speed at r=0.030 m).

(a) Find ω in rad/s and in rpm.
(b) If the read head moves to r=0.058 m (near the outer edge) while maintaining the same 1.2 m/s linear speed, find the new ω. Has ω increased or decreased?

---

**5.** A centrifuge accelerates from rest to 12,000 rpm in 15 s, then maintains that speed for 5.0 minutes, then decelerates to rest in 20 s (all constant angular acceleration/deceleration phases).

(a) Find the angular acceleration during spin-up.
(b) Find the total angular displacement during spin-up (in revolutions).
(c) Find the angular displacement during the constant-speed phase.
(d) Find the angular deceleration during spin-down, and the displacement during that phase.
(e) Find the TOTAL number of revolutions over the entire process.

---

**6.** A wheel of radius 0.25 m has angular velocity 6.0 rad/s and angular acceleration 2.0 rad/s² at a given instant. Find the magnitude and direction (relative to the radius, i.e., angle from purely centripetal) of the total linear acceleration of a point on the rim.

---

### Part B: Torque and Moment of Inertia (Problems 7–14) — 40 pts

**7.** A force of 60 N is applied perpendicular to a door, 0.80 m from the hinges.

(a) Find the torque about the hinges.
(b) If the same force is applied at the same point but at 40° from perpendicular to the door's plane (i.e., the force makes a 40° angle with the door surface... more precisely, assume the angle between the force and the lever arm r⃗ is 40° from the perpendicular direction, so φ=50° from r⃗), find the new torque. *(Clarify: use τ = rF sinφ with φ measured from r⃗ to F⃗; if the force deviates 40° from the perpendicular-to-door direction, then φ = 90°−40° = 50°.)*
(c) At what angle would the torque be maximum, for fixed r and F?

---

**8.** A uniform rod of mass 2.0 kg and length 1.5 m pivots freely about one end. Find its moment of inertia about that end using the table value (rod about end), and verify using the parallel axis theorem starting from the center-of-mass value.

---

**9.** A solid disk (M=6.0 kg, R=0.35 m) and a thin hoop of the same mass and radius are each subjected to the same net torque of 8.0 N·m.

(a) Find the moment of inertia of each.
(b) Find the angular acceleration of each.
(c) Which spins up faster, and by what factor?

---

**10.** A merry-go-round (modeled as a uniform disk, M=250 kg, R=2.0 m) is pushed by a tangential force of 150 N applied at the rim.

(a) Find the moment of inertia.
(b) Find the angular acceleration.
(c) Starting from rest, how long does it take to reach 0.50 rad/s?
(d) How many revolutions does it make during that time?

---

**11.** A compound object consists of a uniform disk (M=3.0 kg, R=0.20 m) with a point mass (m=1.0 kg) attached at the rim. Find the total moment of inertia about the central axis of the disk. (Use the disk's own I plus the point mass treated via I=mr² at r=R.)

---

**12.** Two masses, $m_1=2.5$ kg and $m_2=4.0$ kg, hang from either side of a pulley of mass $M_p=1.0$ kg and radius 0.12 m (treat the pulley as a uniform disk). Using the massive-pulley method from Lecture 20:

(a) Find the acceleration of the system.
(b) Find the tension on each side of the pulley (they will differ).
(c) Verify: compute the net torque on the pulley using your two tensions, and confirm it equals $I\alpha$ with $\alpha = a/R$.

---

**13.** A flywheel (I = 12.0 kg·m², initially at rest) is spun up by a constant torque of 30 N·m.

(a) Find the angular acceleration.
(b) Find the time to reach 100 rad/s.
(c) Find the number of revolutions completed during that time.
(d) Find the work done by the torque during spin-up (Hint: for constant torque, W=τΔθ — this is the rotational analog of W=FΔx; verify your answer equals the flywheel's final rotational KE).

---

**14. (Synthesis)** A uniform rod of length 1.2 m and mass 3.0 kg is pivoted at one end and released from rest in a horizontal position, swinging down under gravity (like a falling door or drawbridge).

(a) Find the moment of inertia about the pivot (rod about end).
(b) Find the torque due to gravity when the rod is horizontal. (Treat gravity as acting at the center of mass, at the rod's midpoint, r=L/2 from the pivot; the force is perpendicular to the rod when the rod is horizontal.)
(c) Find the angular acceleration at the instant of release.
(d) Note: as the rod swings down, the torque changes (it depends on the angle), so you CANNOT use the constant-α kinematic equations for the full swing. This problem will be revisited with energy methods in the next problem set section.

### Part C: Rotational Energy and Rolling (Problems 15–20) — 30 pts

**15.** A solid sphere (M=2.0 kg, R=0.15 m) rotates at ω=12 rad/s about an axis through its center.

(a) Find its moment of inertia.
(b) Find its rotational kinetic energy.

---

**16.** A solid cylinder (M=4.0 kg, R=0.20 m) rolls without slipping at $v_{cm}=3.0$ m/s.

(a) Find ω.
(b) Find the translational KE, rotational KE, and total KE.
(c) What fraction of the total KE is rotational?

---

**17.** A solid sphere, a solid cylinder, and a hoop (all mass M, radius R) are released from rest at the top of the same incline of height h=1.8 m.

(a) Find the speed of each at the bottom using $v=\sqrt{2gh/(1+\beta)}$.
(b) Rank them by speed and by arrival time (fastest to slowest).
(c) If the incline were frictionless (so NONE of the objects could roll — they'd all slide instead), find the common speed all three would have at the bottom, and compare to your answers in (a). Which is larger, and why?

---

**18.** A bowling ball (treat as a solid sphere, M=6.0 kg, R=0.11 m) rolls without slipping at 2.5 m/s on a horizontal lane.

(a) Find its total kinetic energy.
(b) If the ball then rolls up a frictionless ramp (imagine it transitions smoothly, maintaining its rotation since there's no torque to change ω once no friction acts and it's already rotating at the right rate to roll)... Actually, for this problem: assume the ball continues rolling without slipping up an incline that DOES have enough friction to maintain rolling. Find the maximum height it reaches before momentarily stopping (both translation AND rotation stop, since rolling without slipping means ω=0 when v=0).

---

**19. (Synthesis — energy + rolling + incline)** A hollow spherical shell (β=2/3, M=1.5 kg, R=0.10 m) starts from rest at the top of a 30° incline and rolls without slipping a distance of 3.0 m down the slope.

(a) Find the height dropped, h = L sinθ.
(b) Find the speed at the bottom using energy conservation.
(c) Find the acceleration using $a=g\sin\theta/(1+\beta)$, then verify the speed using kinematics ($v^2=2aL$).
(d) Find the minimum coefficient of static friction required to prevent slipping. (This requires the force/torque method: $f_s = \beta Ma_{cm}$ from Lecture 21's derivation, and comparing to $\mu_s N = \mu_s Mg\cos\theta$.)

---

**20. (Comprehensive synthesis)** A yo-yo can be modeled as a uniform disk (M=0.20 kg, R=0.025 m for the axle where the string is wound, but the yo-yo's outer body has effective radius $R_{body}=0.035$ m for its moment of inertia — treat the yo-yo as a uniform disk of radius $R_{body}$ for I purposes, i.e., $I=\frac{1}{2}MR_{body}^2$, but the string unwinds from the smaller axle radius $R_{axle}$). The yo-yo is released from rest and "rolls" down its own string (the string is analogous to the incline's friction — it enforces the no-slip condition at radius $R_{axle}$, not $R_{body}$).

(a) Using energy conservation with the modified rolling condition $v_{cm}=R_{axle}\omega$ (not $R_{body}\omega$!), derive the general formula for the yo-yo's descent speed after falling height h: $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{2gh}{1+I/(MR_{axle}^2)}}$.
(b) Compute $I/(MR_{axle}^2)$ using the given values (note this will be a LARGE number, since $R_{axle} \ll R_{body}$).
(c) Find the speed after falling h=0.50 m.
(d) Explain physically why a yo-yo descends so much more slowly than a ball of the same mass falling freely from the same height — connect this to the large effective β you computed in (b).
