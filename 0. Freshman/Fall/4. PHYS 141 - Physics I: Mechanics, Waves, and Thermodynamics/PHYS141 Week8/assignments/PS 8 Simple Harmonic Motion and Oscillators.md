## Part A — SHM Kinematics (25 points)

**1.** *(5 pts)* A 0.750 kg mass on a spring of $k = 30.0$ N/m is displaced 8.00 cm from equilibrium
and released from rest. Find $\omega$, $T$, $f$, $v_{\max}$, $a_{\max}$, and the total energy.

**2.** *(5 pts)* An oscillator has $A = 0.120$ m and $T = 0.500$ s. Find its speed and acceleration
at the instant $x = 0.060$ m.

**3.** *(5 pts)* An SHM oscillator obeys $x(t) = (0.250~\text{m})\cos(12.0t + \pi/3)$. State $A$,
$\omega$, $T$, $f$, $\phi$, and $x(0)$. Find the first time after $t=0$ at which the mass passes
through $x = 0$.

**4.** *(5 pts)* For an oscillator of amplitude $A$, find the displacement at which the speed is
exactly half its maximum value. Express the answer as a multiple of $A$.

**5.** *(5 pts)* Sketch $x(t)$, $v(t)$, and $a(t)$ for one full cycle of an oscillator released from
rest at $x = +A$. Mark the points where each is zero and where each is extremal, and state the phase
relationships between the three curves.

## Part B — Energy in SHM (20 points)

**6.** *(5 pts)* For the oscillator of Problem 1, find the kinetic and potential energies at
$x = 4.00$ cm, and verify they sum to the total.

**7.** *(5 pts)* At what displacement (as a fraction of $A$) is the kinetic energy exactly equal to
the potential energy?

**8.** *(5 pts)* An oscillator's amplitude is doubled. State by what factor each of the following
changes: period, frequency, maximum speed, maximum acceleration, and total energy.

**9.** *(5 pts)* Show algebraically that $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$ follows from conservation of
energy, starting from $\tfrac12mv^2 + \tfrac12kx^2 = \tfrac12kA^2$.

---

## Part C — Pendulums (25 points)

**10.** *(5 pts)* Find the length of a simple pendulum whose period is exactly 1.00 s on Earth.

**11.** *(5 pts)* A pendulum of length 0.750 m is released from $12°$. Find its period using the
small-angle formula, and estimate the fractional error the approximation introduces.

**12.** *(5 pts)* A uniform rod of length 0.800 m swings from one end. Find its period and its
equivalent simple-pendulum length. Explain why it swings faster than a simple pendulum of the same
length.

**13.** *(5 pts)* A uniform disc of radius 0.150 m is pivoted at a point on its rim. Using
$I_{\text{cm}} = \tfrac12mR^2$ and the parallel-axis theorem, find $I$ about the pivot and hence the
period of small oscillations.

**14.** *(5 pts)* A pendulum clock whose pendulum is 0.9940 m long loses 30 s per day. Should the bob
be raised or lowered, and by how much? Explain your reasoning before computing.

---

## Part D — Damping and Resonance (30 points)

**15.** *(6 pts)* An oscillator has $m = 0.250$ kg, $k = 100$ N/m, and $b = 0.500$ kg/s. Find
$\omega_0$, $\gamma$, $\omega_d$, and $Q$. Classify the damping regime, showing the comparison you
used.

**16.** *(6 pts)* For the oscillator of Problem 15, how long until the amplitude falls to 25% of its
initial value? How long until the **energy** does? Explain why the two answers differ by a factor of
two.

**17.** *(5 pts)* What value of $b$ would critically damp the oscillator of Problem 15? State what
critical damping achieves that neither under- nor overdamping does.

**18.** *(6 pts)* A driven oscillator has $\omega_0 = 40.0$ rad/s, $\gamma = 1.50$ s⁻¹, and
$F_0/m = 2.00$ m/s². Compute the steady-state amplitude at $\omega = 20.0$, $38.0$, $40.0$, and
$42.0$ rad/s. Identify the resonance and comment on the sharpness of the peak.

**19.** *(4 pts)* Explain, in terms of energy transfer per cycle, **why** the response is largest when
$\omega \approx \omega_0$.

**20.** *(3 pts)* A car's shock absorbers have worn out. State whether the suspension is now under-
or overdamped, what the driver feels, and why manufacturers target critical damping.
