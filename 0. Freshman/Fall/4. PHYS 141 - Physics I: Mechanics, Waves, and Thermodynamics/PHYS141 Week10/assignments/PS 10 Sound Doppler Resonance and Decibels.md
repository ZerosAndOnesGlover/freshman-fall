# PHYS 141 · Problem Set 10
## Sound: Doppler Effect, Resonance, and Decibels

**Released:** Friday EOD, Week 10 | **Due:** Friday, Week 11, start of class
**Total: 100 points**

---

**Instructions**
- Show all working. A bare numerical answer earns at most half marks.
- Take $v_{\text{sound}} = 343$ m/s and $I_0 = 1.00\times10^{-12}$ W/m² unless told otherwise.
- **For every Doppler answer, state whether the shift should be up or down before computing, then
  check your result agrees.**
- Give answers to 3 significant figures.

---

## Part A — Sound Waves and Intensity (25 points)

**1.** *(4 pts)* Find the speed of sound in air at $15.0°$C and at $35.0°$C. State why pressure does
not appear in the calculation.

**2.** *(5 pts)* A source emits 2.00 W uniformly in all directions. Find the intensity and the sound
level at 3.00 m.

**3.** *(4 pts)* A sound has intensity $4.00\times10^{-6}$ W/m². Find its level in dB.

**4.** *(4 pts)* A sound level rises from 65.0 dB to 85.0 dB. By what factor has the intensity
increased? By roughly what factor does it *sound* louder?

**5.** *(5 pts)* At 5.00 m from a point source the level is 78.0 dB. Find the level at 20.0 m and the
acoustic power of the source.

**6.** *(3 pts)* A 500 Hz tone has wavelength 0.686 m. Deduce the air temperature.

---

## Part B — Decibels and Perception (15 points)

**7.** *(4 pts)* Two identical machines each produce 70.0 dB at your position. What is the level with
both running? Explain why the answer is not 140 dB.

**8.** *(4 pts)* Show that doubling the intensity always adds 3.01 dB, whatever the starting level.

**9.** *(4 pts)* You move from 2.00 m to 8.00 m from a source. By how many dB does the level fall?
Derive the general "6 dB per doubling of distance" rule.

**10.** *(3 pts)* Explain why sound is measured on a logarithmic scale, referring both to the range of
audible intensities and to how the ear responds.

---

## Part C — Air Columns and Resonance (30 points)

**11.** *(5 pts)* An open–open pipe is 0.750 m long. Find its first three harmonics.

**12.** *(5 pts)* A closed–open pipe is 0.750 m long. Find its first three harmonics and compare with
Problem 11. State the ratio of the two fundamentals.

**13.** *(5 pts)* A closed–open pipe has a fundamental of 220 Hz. Find its length and its next two
harmonics.

**14.** *(6 pts)* A resonance tube driven by a 384 Hz fork resonates when the air column is 0.216 m
and again at 0.663 m. Find the wavelength, the speed of sound, and the end correction.

**15.** *(5 pts)* An open pipe 0.400 m long has radius 12.0 mm. Find its fundamental with and without
the end correction, and the percentage difference.

**16.** *(4 pts)* Explain why a clarinet overblows to a twelfth while a flute overblows to an octave.
Relate your answer to the boundary conditions at each end of the instrument.

---

## Part D — The Doppler Effect (30 points)

**17.** *(5 pts)* A train sounds a 440 Hz horn while approaching at 30.0 m/s. Find the frequency
heard by a stationary observer before and after it passes. State the total drop in pitch.

**18.** *(5 pts)* You drive at 25.0 m/s directly towards a stationary 500 Hz siren. What frequency do
you hear? What if you drive away at the same speed?

**19.** *(5 pts)* A car approaches a source at 20.0 m/s while the source moves towards the car at
15.0 m/s. The source emits 800 Hz. Find the received frequency.

**20.** *(5 pts)* A stationary observer hears 1050 Hz from a siren approaching at 22.0 m/s. Find the
siren's true frequency.

**21.** *(6 pts)* A bat emits 45.0 kHz and flies at 8.00 m/s directly towards a stationary wall.
Find (a) the frequency arriving at the wall and (b) the frequency the bat hears reflected back.
Explain why **two** Doppler shifts are involved, and compute the beat frequency the bat detects
against its own call.

**22.** *(4 pts)* Explain why a moving source and a moving observer travelling at the same speed
produce **different** frequency shifts, and state what is different for light.
