## 1. Academic Calendar Framework

Each academic year consists of:
- **Fall Semester**: 15 instructional weeks + 1 finals week (late August – mid December)
- **Winter Break**: ~3 weeks (mid December – early January)
- **Spring Semester**: 15 instructional weeks + 1 finals week (mid January – early May)
- **Summer Session** (optional): 8-week accelerated session (June – July)

### Key Annual Dates
| Event | Approximate Timing |
|-------|--------------------|
| Fall semester begins | Last Monday of August |
| Labor Day (no classes) | First Monday of September |
| Fall Break | 2 days mid-October |
| Thanksgiving Break | Wednesday–Sunday before last Thursday of November |
| Last day of fall classes | Second Friday of December |
| Fall finals week | Third week of December |
| Spring semester begins | Third Monday of January |
| Martin Luther King Day (no classes) | Third Monday of January |
| Spring Break | One week mid-March |
| Last day of spring classes | First Friday of May |
| Spring finals week | Second week of May |
| Commencement | Third Saturday of May |

## 3. Attendance Policy

Regular attendance is expected in all courses. Faculty set specific attendance policies in their syllabi, subject to these minimums:

- **Lecture courses**: Students missing more than 20% of sessions (3 weeks equivalent) may be assigned a failing grade.
- **Lab sections**: Attendance is mandatory. See Lab Attendance Policy.
- **Excused absences**: Medical emergencies (doctor's note), family emergencies (Dean's documentation), university-sponsored events (advance notice required).

### Lab Attendance Policy
| Unexcused Lab Absences | Consequence |
|------------------------|-------------|
| 1 | Written warning |
| 2 | Final course grade reduced by one letter |
| 3 | Assigned grade of F for the course |

## 4. Academic Integrity

### What Constitutes a Violation
- Submitting work not your own (plagiarism, contract cheating)
- Copying or sharing code for individual assignments
- Unauthorized collaboration on exams
- Using AI tools where prohibited (clearly stated per assignment)
- Falsifying experimental data or results
- Unauthorized use of exam materials from previous semesters

### Permitted AI Use (unless otherwise stated)
- Asking AI for conceptual explanations ✓
- Using AI to check grammar/prose ✓
- Using AI to generate code you submit ✗
- Using AI during timed assessments ✗

### Consequences
| Offense | Consequence |
|---------|-------------|
| First offense (minor) | Zero on the assessment + educational module |
| First offense (major) | Failing grade for the course |
| Second offense | Academic suspension (1 semester minimum) |
| Third offense | Permanent expulsion |

## 5. Late Work Policy

### Standard Policy (unless course syllabus overrides)
| Submission Timing | Penalty |
|------------------|---------|
| On time | No penalty |
| < 24 hours late | −10% |
| 24–48 hours late | −20% |
| 48–72 hours late | −30% |
| > 72 hours late | Zero credit |

**Extensions**: Request at least 48 hours before the deadline (except genuine emergencies). Extensions are not guaranteed and are at instructor discretion.

### Items Not Eligible for Late Submission
- In-class quizzes (no make-up unless excused absence)
- Midterm and final examinations
- Live presentations and demos

## 6. Exam Policies

### Before the Exam
- Arrive at least 10 minutes early
- Bring university ID (required for all exams)
- No electronic devices unless explicitly permitted
- Permitted materials will be specified per exam (cheat sheet, calculator, etc.)

### During the Exam
- No leaving the room in the first 15 or last 10 minutes
- Mobile phones must be silenced and face-down or in a bag
- Questions: raise your hand — do not approach the proctor

### After the Exam
- Re-grade requests: submit in writing to instructor within **7 calendar days** of grade release
- Re-grade policy: the **entire** submission is re-evaluated; grade may go up or down

## 7. Office Hours

All instructors are required to hold a minimum of **4 office hours per week**. TAs are required to hold a minimum of **6 office hours per week** per course section they support.

Office hours are posted in the course syllabus and on the department portal. Students do not need appointments for regular office hours. Appointments may be scheduled for extended or private discussions.

**Remote office hours**: Available via video conference (Zoom links posted on course portal). In-person office hours are not guaranteed to have parallel Zoom access.

---

## 8. Disability Accommodations

Students requiring accommodations must register with the **Office of Disability Services (ODS)** before the end of Week 2 of each semester. Accommodation letters must be presented to each instructor individually. Common accommodations include:
- Extended time on exams (1.5× or 2×)
- Distraction-reduced testing environment
- Note-taking assistance
- Alternative assignment formats

---

## 9. Required Technology

All CSE students are expected to own or have reliable access to:
- A personal laptop (Linux, macOS, or Windows with WSL2) — minimum 8GB RAM, 256GB storage
- University-provided cloud computing credits (AWS Educate / GitHub Education) for heavy workloads
- The department provides access to high-performance GPU clusters for Year 3–4 courses

Software is free through university licenses or open-source. A complete setup guide is provided at course start.
