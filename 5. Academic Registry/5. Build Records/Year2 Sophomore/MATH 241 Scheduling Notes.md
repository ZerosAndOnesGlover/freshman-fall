# MATH 241 Scheduling Notes

What had to be decided before MATH 241 could be written, and why each decision went the way it did.
Written 2026-09-09, while building Weeks 0 and 1. **The authority** is
`5. Academic Registry/1. Scheduling/Year2 - Sophomore/CSE_Year2_Sophomore_Curriculum.docx`, and
where the registry's own scheduling files settle a question, they do.

---

## 1. The curriculum states no weights, so the registry's stand

The docx's MATH 241 entry gives credits (4), semester, prerequisite (MATH 141), a relevance line,
a course description, three textbooks and thirteen weekly topics — **and no assessment line at
all.** CS 201, PROG 201, CS 211 and every Spring course except MATH 251 and ECE 211 have one.

[[Year2 - Sophomore/MASTER TIMETABLE|MASTER TIMETABLE]] gives *Problem Sets 35%, Midterms 40%,
Final 25%*, which sums to 100 and is the only other complete statement for this course.
**It is adopted**, following the precedent ECE 110 set in Year 1 for exactly the same gap, and it is
already what [[MATH 241]] and [[Year2 - Sophomore/ASSESSMENT CALENDAR|ASSESSMENT CALENDAR]] record.

Split into per-item weights for the gradebook: PS 35 (lowest of 13 dropped), Midterm 1 20,
Midterm 2 20, Final 25. Nothing was invented; the 40 was halved because the registry names two
midterms of equal standing.

Recorded as a deviation in [[MATH241 Week0/resources/Course Overview Syllabus|Course Overview Syllabus]]
so a reader meets it without coming here.

## 2. Week 0 has no Monday lecture, because the Monday is Labor Day

[[Year2 - Sophomore/ROOM ASSIGNMENTS|ROOM ASSIGNMENTS]] puts this course's lectures on
**Mon/Tue/Fri 11:00–11:50 in SSB 110**. [[ACADEMIC CALENDAR]] runs Fall Week 0 from **Wed Aug 27 to
Fri Sep 5** and marks **Mon Sep 1 as Labor Day, no classes, still Week 0.**

The slots inside that ten-day week are therefore:

| Day | | |
|---|---|---|
| Fri Aug 29 | ✅ | **L01** |
| Mon Sep 1 | ❌ | Labor Day |
| Tue Sep 2 | ✅ | **L02** |
| Fri Sep 5 | ✅ | **L03** |

**Three usable slots for three lectures, so nothing is lost** — but they are sat Friday, Tuesday,
Friday rather than Monday, Tuesday, Friday, and every Week 0 lecture file states its own day in the
header. From Week 1 the pattern is the ordinary Mon/Tue/Fri.

> **Only this course is affected.** CS 201 (Mon/Wed/Fri) also loses its Monday, but its Week 0 has
> Wed Aug 27, Fri Aug 29, Wed Sep 3 and Fri Sep 5 available — four slots for three lectures — so it
> absorbs the loss without comment. PROG 201 (Tue/Wed/Thu) and CS 211 (Tue/Thu) never had a Monday.
> **MATH 241 is the only Year 2 course whose Week 0 lecture days move**, which is why it is recorded
> here rather than in the registry.

## 3. The recitation lags a full week, and the lag puts it where it is useful

[[Year2 - Sophomore/ROOM ASSIGNMENTS|ROOM ASSIGNMENTS]] gives **Thu 15:00–15:50, SSB 108**, and
[[Year2 - Sophomore/FALL SCHEDULE|FALL SCHEDULE]] describes it as *"working through problem set
questions with TAs — attend with questions ready."*

**Thursday falls before this course's Friday lecture**, so a session covering Week *N* cannot be sat
in Week *N* — the week's teaching is not finished. This is the same test
[[Year2 - Sophomore/ASSESSMENT CALENDAR|ASSESSMENT CALENDAR]] applies to labs, and it gives the same
answer: **Recitation *N* covers Week *N* and is sat on the Thursday of Week *N+1*.**

**The lag is also the right answer independently.** Problem sets are released Wednesday of their own
week and due 17:00 Friday of the week after, so Recitation *N* lands **the afternoon before PS *N*
is due**, with all three of that week's lectures behind it and the paper in the student's hand. A
session sat a week earlier would have neither.

**The slots count out exactly.** Thursdays of Weeks 1–12 give twelve sittings for Recitations 0–11,
and Recitation 12 — the final review — takes the Thursday of the completion period. **Thirteen
recitations, thirteen slots, no make-ups needed.** Nothing collides: the Thursday of Week 6 is
usable (Fall Break is that week's *Monday*), and Thanksgiving recess falls in the gap between
Weeks 11 and 12 rather than inside a teaching week.

> **This is the third of four Year 2 Fall courses to lag, and the disagreement is real.** CS 201
> (Tue lab, Mon/Wed/Fri lectures) lags; PROG 201 (Mon lab, Tue/Wed/Thu lectures) lags a full week;
> MATH 241 lags; **CS 211 does not**, because its Friday lab has all of its week's teaching behind
> it. A student takes all four. Every course states its own rule in its syllabus and in every
> session file's header, and each of the four says so explicitly rather than assuming the habit
> transfers.

**There is no recitation in Week 0.** Its two Thursdays are Aug 28 — before this course's first
lecture has happened at all — and Sep 4, which precedes L03. There is also no problem set to work
through yet. **Unlike the labs, MATH 241 holds no Week 0 setup session**, because there is nothing
to set up: no machine, no toolchain, no account.

## 4. The registry lists no TA for this course, and that is an omission

[[Year2 - Sophomore/OFFICE HOURS|OFFICE HOURS]] gives a named TA with hours for CS 201 (Felix
Oduya), CS 211 (Ngozi Eze) and PROG 201 (Aisha Mohammed), **and none for MATH 241** — only
Prof. Ruth Abara's own hours, Mon 13:00–15:00 and Thu 10:00–11:00 in SSB 310.

Meanwhile [[Year2 - Sophomore/FALL SCHEDULE|FALL SCHEDULE]] describes the Thursday session as run

**Nothing was invented to close the gap.** No TA name appears anywhere in the course material. The
syllabus records that the recitation is run by Prof. Abara until a TA is assigned, and points at the
Engineering Help Desk in BH 120 — which is staffed for all Year 1–2 courses and is open
Mon–Thu 10:00–20:00 — for drop-in help.

**This is recorded as an omission, not a decision.** If the department assigns one, the syllabus's
Schedule table and the two recitation sheets are the three places to update.

> **Worth noting alongside it:** Prof. Abara's Thursday office hour is **10:00–11:00, the morning
> before the 15:00 recitation and the morning before each problem set is due.** That is a good
> accident and the recitation sheets point at it.

## 5. Quiz 6 cannot be sat on a Monday

Quizzes run ten minutes into each course's **first lecture of the week**, Weeks 1–11, and for this
course that is Monday. **The Monday of Week 6 is Fall Break.**

**Quiz 6 therefore moves to the Tuesday of Week 6**, which is that week's first MATH 241 lecture. It
still covers Week 5, and it is the only quiz in the term that is not a Monday. Nothing else moves,
and no quiz is dropped — Weeks 1–11 still carry eleven of them, as
[[_MATH 241 Quiz Record]] lists.

*(Recorded now, while the convention is being set, rather than discovered in Week 6. The Week 6
paper will carry it in its header, as PROG 201's Lab 5 does for the same break.)*

## 6. Every number in Weeks 0 and 1 was computed, and the programs ship with the notes

The reference machine is the same one PROG 201 used: **Intel i5-8250U, Ubuntu 24.04.4 LTS,
kernel 7.0**, with **CPython 3.14.2** and IEEE 754 binary64.

**NumPy and SciPy are not installed on it**, which turned out to be a benefit rather than a
constraint. Both scripts are **pure Python with no imports beyond `fractions`, `random` and
`time`**, so a student reproduces them on any machine with a Python interpreter, and the exact
results are exact rather than dependent on a BLAS.

| File | Reproduces |
|---|---|
| `MATH241 Week0/resources/elimination.py` | L01–L03: the running example and its pivots; the pivoting failure at four values of $\varepsilon$; exact $\operatorname{cond}_\infty$ of the Hilbert matrices and the float solve error at each; the operation-count table; the 299-digit pivot |
| `MATH241 Week1/resources/matrices.py` | L04–L06: $PQ \ne QP$; the $(AB)C$ / $A(BC)$ flop count **and timing**; $A = LU$ and $A^{-1}$ of the running matrix; solve-against-invert on Hilbert systems; the tridiagonal $K$ and its dense inverse |

**Exact results were computed in `Fraction` and floating-point results in `float`**, and the notes
say which is which at every table. **One figure is machine-dependent and is labelled as such:** the
$(AB)C$ / $A(BC)$ wall-clock timing in L04 §5, measured at 1.048 s against 0.001331 s, a speedup of
787 against a flop-count prediction of 750.

**That 787-against-750 gap is kept rather than smoothed.** It is real — the losing bracketing writes
and re-reads an 8 MB intermediate — and the lecture uses it to say that flops are a first-order model
and not the whole cost. **The disagreement is the lesson**, which is the same policy PROG 201 §7
adopted for its shared-memory measurement.

### Two figures worth flagging as load-bearing

- **`0.0` returned for an answer of `1`** (L03 §4, $\varepsilon = 10^{-17}$). Traced in the notes to
  the exact step: $1 - 10^{17}$ and $2 - 10^{17}$ round to the same `double`. **The lecture states
  the mechanism in full rather than deferring to CS 201**, which teaches IEEE 754 in *its* Week 1 —
  one week after this. The forward reference is marked; nothing in L03 depends on it.
- **The solve-against-invert penalty is not monotone** (L05 §6): $20\times$ at $n=6$, $2\times$ at
  $n=10$, $91\times$ at $n=12$. **This is reported as irregular rather than tidied into a trend**,
  because the irregularity is the argument — an unpredictable penalty cannot be budgeted for, and
  that is a better reason not to invert than a predictable one would be.

## 7. The site builder had no "recitation" section, and now does

MATH 241 is **the first course in the vault with a recitation rather than a laboratory**, so its
week folders contain `recitation/` where every other course has `lab/`.

`site/build.py` maps directory names to display sections through `SECTION_LABELS` and orders them
with `SECTION_ORDER`. An unrecognised directory still builds — it falls through to `describe_dir`'s
generic `folder` branch — but it is titled by `clean_title` rather than by the table and **sorts to
position 99, after `solutions_instructor`**, which would have put the recitation after the
instructor solutions in every week index.

Two lines were added: `"recitation": "Recitation"` to `SECTION_LABELS`, and `"recitation"` into
`SECTION_ORDER` between `labs` and `assignments` — the slot a lab would have occupied. **Nothing
else in the builder was touched**, and `python3 site/build.py` builds 2,938 pages clean.

MATH 251 in Spring has a Wednesday recitation and will use the same directory name.

## 8. Not fixed here: path-qualified wikilinks do not resolve in the built site

`build_link_resolver` in `site/build.py` indexes pages **by file stem only**. A link written
`[[MATH241 Week0/resources/Reading Guide Week 0|Reading Guide Week 0]]` therefore renders as
`wikilink broken`, while the same target written `[[Reading Guide Week 0]]` resolves.

**This is pre-existing and vault-wide, not introduced by MATH 241.** The same ten-odd forms break in
PROG 201's built pages, in CS 201's, and for the registry's own
`[[Year2 - Sophomore/COURSE POLICIES]]` — which every Year 2 course links that way. Obsidian
resolves all of them; the static builder does not.

**MATH 241 uses the same convention as the courses around it** rather than diverging, so that the
fix — teaching the resolver to match on a path suffix as well as a stem — fixes every course at
once. **Left alone deliberately:** it is a change to link resolution across 2,938 pages, it can
introduce ambiguity where two courses share a filename, and it is not MATH 241's to make while
building two weeks of content. Recorded so that whoever does it knows the scope.
